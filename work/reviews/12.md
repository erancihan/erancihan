# Review — chapter 12 (Gameplay systems)

**Persona:** gameplay engineer + the ASan operator.

**Verified:** 50.1% code, 22 blocks, largest 19 added lines, 100% anchored, lint
clean. All four system headers plus `GameState.hpp` and `Game.hpp` reconstruct
**exactly**; `Game.cpp` differs only by ch13's two HUD lines. Reconstructed tree
builds and runs; screenshot shows the playable game — twin bolt streams from the
wingtips (fired via synthetic keydown), a red chaser and an amber drifter inbound,
validation silent.

**The derivation is the guide's best-evidenced one.** The naive WeaponSystem —
holding `Transform* t` across `fireBolt` — was actually compiled and run both
ways on this machine: plain build prints "bolts spawned: 2" and looks fine; the
ASan build reports `heap-use-after-free`, with the trace descending through
`Math::forward` into the freed Transform array. The chapter's quoted report is
that real output, lightly trimmed. The fix is chapter 04's prescription (copy
before spawning), and `EnemySystem::update` then applies the same lesson
unprompted (`Transform playerT = *pt;`), which is fading done right: derive once,
expect the reader to recognise it the second time.

**What's right**
- Spawn-band numbers tied to bolt reach and the far plane rather than presented
  as taste; spawn cone in the player's own axes, linked to the camera-rig and
  muzzle-offset pattern.
- The `clamp`-before-`acos` NaN is explained with its downstream blast radius
  (poisoned quaternion → poisoned matrix → smeared triangle), and the symptom
  table carries the matching row.
- Pre-multiply vs post-multiply mirrored against chapter 10 explicitly: which
  side the delta goes on is which frame you mean it in.
- The `en->health <= 0.0f` guard is derived from deferred destruction (dead
  entities persist until the flush), preventing double-score.
- Layer bits used where general (bolt→enemy), skipped where singular
  (ship→enemy), and the challenge shows the exact future (enemy bullets) where
  the general path becomes mandatory.
- Deliberate breakage (200 enemies at 0.05s spawns) teaches that the cap is a
  design knob, not a performance knob.

**Findings**
1. *(fixed)* Two diffs ended `+}` where the enclosing function's original brace
   already sat after the anchor — caught by the exact-match comparison as one
   stray brace per file. Both became context lines. Same class as ch09's include
   duplication: replay-verification sees what compilation forgives.
2. *(fixed)* Two blocks over the cap (23, 24) split at real seams — constants
   from skeleton, `cull` from `update`.

**Score: 9.5/10**
