# Review — chapter 05.A (Swapchain, depth & render pass)

**Persona:** Vulkan practitioner + pedagogy reviewer.

**Verified:** 100% anchored, lint clean, largest block 20. `Swapchain.hpp` (27) and
`Swapchain.cpp` (208 code lines) reconstruct **exactly**.

**What's right**
- The derivation is the `minImageCount + 1`: the reader writes the minimum, is told
  precisely why the CPU stalls ("run out of paper to write on"), and the checkpoint
  *prints the count* so the change is observable rather than asserted.
- Real traps flagged where they occur, not in a footnote: `glfwGetFramebufferSize`
  vs `glfwGetWindowSize` on HiDPI (renders fine, looks soft, no error); re-reading
  `imageCount` from the driver rather than trusting your request; `maxImageCount == 0`
  meaning "no limit" so the clamp needs a guard; `glfwWaitEvents` not `glfwPollEvents`
  in the minimise loop, or you spin a core.
- `storeOp = DONT_CARE` on depth gets the tiled-GPU explanation, which turns an
  enum into a decision.
- The deliberate breakage is the best in the guide so far: swap the attachment
  order, get an error; *then* renumber the references to match and watch the error
  go away. The lesson — the numbering is arbitrary but three places must agree —
  can't be delivered by a one-way break.
- One depth image vs N colour images is read off the member list and justified.

**Findings**
1. **The verifier caught a real defect.** The "replace the image-count line" diff
   had only `-`/`+` lines and no context, so the anchor was unlocatable — and
   because replay is cumulative, that one bad block cascaded into 15 downstream
   anchor failures. Added context lines. This is precisely the failure mode the
   brief's §3(a) exists to catch, and it would have shipped undetected under an
   eyeball review.
2. Chapter is long (~740 lines) but Vulkan's swapchain genuinely is; blocks average
   about 10 added lines, so the density is even.

**Score: 9/10** — one real anchoring defect, found and fixed by the checker.
