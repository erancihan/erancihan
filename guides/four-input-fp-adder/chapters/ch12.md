## The Specification, in Full

This is the specification of record for `fp32_add4`. Every clause cites the measurement or proof that backs it, because a clause with no evidence behind it is this guide's recurring defect class — the unverified belief — and a specification is exactly the wrong document to house one. Read it as a contract in both directions: the corner suite later in this chapter is organized as clause discharge, and the out-of-scope list (S10) exists so nobody reads silence as a promise.

---

**Specification: `fp32_add4` — four-input IEEE 754 binary32 adder**

**S1. Operation.** `fp32_add4` computes the sum of four binary32 operands as **three binary32 additions in the fixed tree association `(a+b)+(c+d)`**: level 1 computes `a+b` and `c+d`, level 2 adds the two level-1 results. The association order AND the port-to-operand mapping are normative, not implementation detail: the operand multiset {+max, +max, −max, −max} delivered as `(a,b,c,d) = (max, max, −max, −max)` returns qNaN with flags `invalid+overflow+inexact`, while the same multiset interleaved `(max, −max, max, −max)` returns +0 with no flags — measured this session across all 6 distinct port orders through the tree: 2 orders give qNaN/111, 4 give +0/000 (and the sequential association would give +inf: chapter 10's "One Multiset, Three Answers"). An implementation that reassociates or permutes ports implements a *different function*. Each of the three additions is a complete IEEE 754 addition on binary32 — chapter 9's `fp32_add2`, proven against exact rational arithmetic through chapter 8's chain and this chapter's independent model (see "The Assembly Proofs").

*Why a tree and not a sequential chain* — and honestly not for accuracy: chapter 10's 1.2M-quadruple sweep ("How Often the Shape Matters") found no systematic accuracy winner — per-regime gaps are real but their direction flips between regimes. The tree is chosen because at equal throughput it is 2 dependent additions deep instead of 3, and pipelined it costs latency 4 and 336 register bits against the sequential chain's latency 6 and 540 bits (chapter 11's counted figures).

**S2. Formats.** All four operands and the result are binary32. Subnormal operands and subnormal results are handled exactly — gradual underflow, with the normalize stop at effective exponent 1 (chapter 9; all four of taxonomy row B's cases directed in the suite). There is no flush-to-zero in either direction (S10).

**S3. Rounding.** Rounding attribute: **roundTiesToEven only**, applied **per addition — the result is rounded three times**, once at each internal binary32 boundary. This is deliberately NOT the single-rounding (correctly-rounded four-input) function. Measured this session against the exact single-rounding oracle (50,000 all-finite quadruples per regime, seed 20260821): the composed result equals the correctly rounded sum on **98.35 %** of full-range quadruples but only **67.96 %** (operand exponents within a ±2 window) and **72.10 %** (±10 window) of clustered-exponent quadruples — note the window construction differs from the research notes' 2-wide/10-wide draw, which measures 73.12 %/69.49 % at the same quoted seed; both reproduce, and the spread between constructions is itself the point the next sentence makes; on the acceptance search's zero-heavy adversarial stream it is 99.336 % (990,187 of 996,804). Consistent with chapter 10's "Rounding Once Instead of Three Times", and with its warning intact: which clustered window is *worse* flips between regime constructions and conditioning choices, so no monotone "more clustered = worse" law is claimed — only that clustered traffic misses the correctly rounded sum on a fifth to a third of quadruples. The specification therefore defines the correct answer AS the three-rounding composition, and the golden reference must follow the wiring: chapter 10's "The Reference Follows the Wiring" measured an order-unfaithful reference failing a correct DUT 1,369 times in 10,000, and this chapter's g1 mutation re-measures it at the flagship (17.7 % under the suite's regime mix).

**S4. Flags.** Three output flags: `invalid`, `overflow`, `inexact`. Each is defined as **the OR of that exception over the three component additions** — the IEEE "computed as three separate operations" reading; flags accumulate as status (chapter 10's "Flags Under Composition"). Consequences, all measured:

- A level-1 overflow whose infinity is later absorbed or cancelled still reports `overflow` (chapter 10's Q1-Q3 rows; a final-stage-only flag OR loses it — chapter 10's mutant D3, 12 corner kills).
- `inexact` **over-reports relative to the single-rounding ideal, in one direction only**: on up to ~7 % of *clustered* (win2) quadruples whose composed result bits happen to equal the correctly rounded sum — 7.095 % in chapter 10's win2 regime, 0.362 % at win10, and 0.000 % on full-range traffic (chapter 10's table, the zero re-measured by this chapter's review) — the composition still raises inexact — a stage rounded, even though the final bits landed exact. Provably one-directional; chapter 10's proof plus its reviewer's 900k-vector adversarial hunt found no counterexample in the impossible direction. The flag means "some stage rounded or overflowed", not "the delivered bits are not the exact sum".
- **`underflow` is omitted, with a proof, not a shrug**: in any binary32 addition a result below the normal range is always exact (Sterbenz-style cancellation; 0 inexact in 400,000 cancelling pairs, chapter 7's "The Flags an Adder Can Actually Raise"), and IEEE default underflow signals only when tiny AND inexact — so an adder-only datapath can never raise it. This lifts to the composition, because each stage is an addition.
- `invalid` is raised only by an sNaN operand at any stage or `inf + (−inf)` at any stage. NaN *propagation* is not invalid (chapter 10's N1 row).

**S5. NaN policy.** Any NaN among the operands, or born at any stage, yields a quiet NaN. Outputs are specified by **class and quiet bit, plus measured payload propagation**: each `fp32_add2` propagates `a`'s NaN over `b`'s with the quiet bit forced and payload bits [21:0] preserved, so the composed priority is **a > b > c > d** (measured this session: four distinct qNaN operands return `a`'s payload; NaNs in only `c` and `d` return `c`'s; the sNaN `7FA00077` in `d` returns `7FE00077` plus invalid). The payload rule is a *measured behavior of this implementation*, stated so the golden chain can be bit-exact (chapter 10's "NaN Birth Sites and Signed Zero, Composed"); IEEE 754 leaves it implementation-defined and ports of this design must not rely on it. **The sign of a NaN result is never specified and never compared** — chapter 7 measured the same simulator emitting differently-signed NaNs at elaboration vs runtime; the suite compares NaN class, quiet bit, and payload, never sign.

**S6. Specials and intermediate-event semantics.**

- **Signed zero.** Per stage: `(+0)+(−0) = +0` in either order; same-signed zero pairs keep the sign; exact cancellation of nonzero operands gives +0 (the RNE rule, chapter 5 row A). Composed and measured over all 16 all-zero-operand quadruples: **the result is −0 iff all four operands are −0**; the other 15 give +0; flags all zero.
- **Infinity.** An infinite operand propagates through its level; like-signed infinities add to the same infinity; `inf + (−inf)` at any stage yields qNaN + invalid.
- **Intermediate events poison per the composition, and the spec owns this.** A level-1 overflow manufactures a genuine ±inf which participates in level 2 as a true infinity. Worked, measured example: `(max + max) + (−max + (−1.0))` → level 1: +inf (overflow) and ≈−max; level 2: `+inf + (−max)` = **+inf**, flags 011 — although the exact four-input sum (≈ max − 1) is finite and representable. Likewise `(max+max) + (−max + −max)` → `+inf + (−inf)` = qNaN + invalid — a NaN born two levels deep from four finite operands. Callers who need the single-rounding answer need a different design (S10).
- **`exact_zero` contract clause** (the D9 disposition, this chapter): inside each `fp32_add2`, `fp32_addsub.exact_zero` is significant only under `eff_sub`; its gate's effective-add case is unreachable-except-screened — proven in "The Acceptance: A Proof, Attacked" and witnessed on the wire by the shipped suite.

**S7. Pipeline and interface.** Latency **4 clock cycles**, throughput one quadruple per cycle, fully streaming, no stall or backpressure. `in_valid` qualifies the operand ports each cycle; `out_valid` — the valid pipes chained through the levels — qualifies the outputs. **Outputs in cycles where `out_valid = 0`, the entire fill window included, are unspecified and must be ignored**: chapter 11's "Reset the Control, Not the Datapath" measured fill-window garbage that decodes as a defined +inf with flags, so "looks plausible" is not "valid". Bubbles (in_valid low, X on the data ports) are first-class and are streamed through every proof run in this chapter.

**S8. Reset.** `rst_n` resets **control only** — the valid pipes. The datapath registers free-run and are never cleared: chapter 11 measured that a full datapath reset manufactures a defined-looking +inf/overflow from the illegal all-zeros state during fill, cycle-pinned by its `tb_reset`. Recovery from reset is `out_valid` gating, nothing else.

**S9. Verification obligations bound to this spec** — what "conforms" means: streaming equivalence to the combinational tree AND to the order-faithful golden chain (both ≥ 100k quadruples, "The Assembly Proofs"); the 321-quad corner suite including the D9 witness; the 105-bin coverage model closed with its pinned counts; the regression targets of "The Regression: What Ships and What Soaks".

**S10. Out of scope, stated so nobody reads silence as a promise:** the four other IEEE rounding-direction attributes; flush-to-zero / DAZ; the single-rounding four-input sum and any accuracy claim beyond S3's measured table; the underflow flag (omitted WITH proof, S4); traps and alternate exception handling — flags only; NaN payload portability beyond S5's measured behavior; operand isolation and power gating; backpressure. Timing and Fmax are documentation only — no synthesis tool exists in this environment (chapter 11's "Timing, Fmax, and the Epistemic Wall", maintained).

## fp32_add4: The Assembly

Here is the complete flagship, from `src/ch12/fp32_add4.v`. It is chapter 11's `fp32_add4_tree_p` restated under the flagship name — the header in the shipped file says so, because a guide that teaches provenance tracking should practice it — and it earns the title "complete source" honestly: every arithmetic wire in the design passes through modules whose files this listing's manifest compiles unmodified from three earlier chapters.

```verilog
module fp32_add4
  (input  wire        clk,
   input  wire        rst_n,
   input  wire        in_valid,
   input  wire [31:0] a, b, c, d,
   output wire [31:0] result,
   output wire        invalid,
   output wire        overflow,
   output wire        inexact,
   output wire        out_valid);

  wire [31:0] sum_ab, sum_cd;
  wire        inv_ab, ovf_ab, inx_ab, v_ab;
  wire        inv_cd, ovf_cd, inx_cd, v_cd;
  wire        inv_r,  ovf_r,  inx_r;

  fp32_add2_p2 u_add_ab (.clk(clk), .rst_n(rst_n), .in_valid(in_valid),
                         .a(a), .b(b), .result(sum_ab), .invalid(inv_ab),
                         .overflow(ovf_ab), .inexact(inx_ab),
                         .out_valid(v_ab));
  fp32_add2_p2 u_add_cd (.clk(clk), .rst_n(rst_n), .in_valid(in_valid),
                         .a(c), .b(d), .result(sum_cd), .invalid(inv_cd),
                         .overflow(ovf_cd), .inexact(inx_cd),
                         .out_valid(v_cd));
  fp32_add2_p2 u_add_r  (.clk(clk), .rst_n(rst_n), .in_valid(v_ab),
                         .a(sum_ab), .b(sum_cd), .result(result),
                         .invalid(inv_r), .overflow(ovf_r),
                         .inexact(inx_r), .out_valid(out_valid));

  // Flags are data with a latency: delay the level-1 OR by u_add_r's
  // 2-cycle latency so it retires with its own transaction.
  wire [2:0] f1 = {inv_ab | inv_cd, ovf_ab | ovf_cd, inx_ab | inx_cd};
  reg  [2:0] f1_d1, f1_d2;
  always @(posedge clk) begin
    f1_d1 <= f1;
    f1_d2 <= f1_d1;
  end
  assign invalid  = f1_d2[2] | inv_r;
  assign overflow = f1_d2[1] | ovf_r;
  assign inexact  = f1_d2[0] | inx_r;

endmodule
```

That is the entire design contribution of chapter 12: three instances, one wire, six flops of flag delay, three ORs. The six flops are the composition's one design obligation of its own — chapter 11's "Skew, and the Other Four: Bugs That Pass a Weaker Harness" measured what happens without them (16.0 % of streamed quads retire wearing a neighbor's flags while every data word stays correct), and this chapter's mutation record re-measures it at the flagship: 16.3 % of the shipped streaming target's quads, result-changed = 0. Everything else is inheritance, and the inheritance is the point:

| Layer | Module(s) | From | Status in this chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| field decode | `fp32_fields`, `fp32_class` | ch07 | instantiated verbatim (67.1M-pattern classifier sweep stands) |
| sticky-aware alignment core | `align_sticky` | ch02 | instantiated verbatim inside `fp32_align` |
| the seven golden steps | `fp32_unpack`, `fp32_screen`, `fp32_swap`, `fp32_align`, `fp32_addsub`, `fp32_normalize`, `fp32_round_pack`, composed as `fp32_add2` | ch09 | instantiated verbatim — the only datapath |
| the 2-stage pipeline unit | `fp32_add2_p2` (banks at the 73-bit addsub cut and the 35-bit output cut, 2-bit valid pipe, 110 state bits) | ch11 | instantiated three times |
| the tree shape and its oracles | `fp32_add4_tree`, `ref_add4_tree` | ch10 | the shape restated here; both modules compiled next to this one as oracles |

**Why this depth.** The choice is chapter 11's "Cutting at the Priced Seams" cashed one last time. At one quadruple per cycle, the priced options were: the combinational tree (latency 0, 0 state bits, the whole two-level cloud in one cycle); this design (3 × 110 + 6 = **336 state bits, latency 4**); the p4-per-unit tree (3 × 284 + 12 = 864 bits, latency 8); the pipelined sequential chain (540 bits, latency 6, counted in chapter 11). No synthesis tool exists in this environment, so no option can claim a nanosecond — the epistemic wall stands — and the selection therefore maximizes what *is* measurable: the p2 cut sits at chapter 9's cheapest priced seams and costs the fewest registers of any pipelined option. The p4 upgrade is the documented option for a reader who has a synthesis target and measures the stage depths this guide cannot.

**The port mapping is normative, and the RTL is where you see it.** `u_add_ab` takes `a` and `b`, so the pairing happens at the ports: the interleaved delivery of the S1 multiset hands each level-1 adder a `max, −max` pair — two exact cancellations, +0 and +0, and a +0 result — while the tree-order delivery hands each a like-signed pair, overflows both into infinities of opposite sign, and manufactures `+inf + (−inf)` at level 2. Same multiset, same module, qNaN/111 against +0/000 — measured across all six distinct port orders this session (2 give qNaN, 4 give +0). The corner suite carries all six as its MULTI family, so the spec's strangest clause is regression-guarded, not narrated.

## The Assembly Proofs

An assembled design earns nothing from its parts' pedigrees until the assembly itself is proven, so the first two shipped testbenches are streaming-equivalence sweeps — chapter 11's "The Streaming-Equivalence Harness" retargeted verbatim, with its whole discipline intact: a new quadruple every cycle, the expectation in a LAT-deep shared-index scoreboard, `!==` behind an X-guard, `out_valid` checked with `===` on every cycle including fill, X-data bubbles as first-class traffic, a drain under X, count guards that make a truncated run fail, seed once and discard the first draw. Two sweeps, two different oracles, deliberately disjoint seeds:

- **`tb_add4_stream`** compares the pipelined flagship against chapter 10's *combinational* `fp32_add4_tree` in the same simulation, seed 9090 — same modules, no registers, so any divergence is a pipelining bug by construction.
- **`tb_add4_gold`** compares it against **`ref_add4_tree`** — three of chapter 8's `fp32_add_alg` golden adders wired in the tree's exact association, seed 777214 — an oracle sharing *no RTL* with the datapath, compared on the full 32-bit result (NaN payloads included) plus all three flags. This is the only bench in the repo that checks the pipelined flagship against the golden chain directly, and it is what lets S9 say "conforms" rather than "matches itself".

Both ship in the manifest at 60k-quad size and were re-run this session at the 101k headline size out of harness:

```
PASS tb_add4_stream (101019 valid quads incl 16 directed, 1000 bubbles, 1 result/cycle, latency 4)
PASS tb_add4_gold (101013 valid quads incl 16 directed, 1006 bubbles, 1 result/cycle, latency 4)
```

Zero mismatches, both oracles — and the equivalence chain closes end to end: pipelined flagship → combinational tree (this section) → order-faithful golden chain (chapter 10's 240,000-quadruple record, and directly again here) → `fp32_add_alg` → exact rational arithmetic (chapter 8's 1,004,425-pair record). The 16 directed quads at the head of each stream place flaggy traffic immediately adjacent to benign traffic on purpose; that adjacency is what kills flag-skew mutants, and the mutation record shows it doing so.

One more link was added this session, because an equivalence sweep proves "same as", never "correct" — chapter 9's lesson from "Composition, and the Golden-Equivalence Sweep". The chapter's research pass wrote **`oracle.py`**, a spec-level exact model built from the specification text (per-stage `fractions.Fraction` arithmetic, hand-rolled roundTiesToEven, the S5/S6 screen semantics, per-stage flags OR-composed) rather than from any RTL, and this session it was re-verified against the RTL's own transcripts: **1,000,064 pairs and 1,000,256 quadruples, results AND flags, zero mismatches**. Two independently written implementations of the specification agreeing at the two-million-vector level is the strongest correctness statement this guide's toolchain can make: agreement of implementations that share nothing but the written spec. `oracle.py` ships in `src/ch12/` — it is also the pinning authority for everything the corner suite and coverage model expect.

## D9: The History of a Debt

Every verification methodology eventually meets a case that tests whether its practitioners believe it, and this guide's case has a name. **D9** is chapter 10's mutation-record designation for chapter 9's B-M2: `fp32_addsub`'s `exact_zero` output with its `eff_sub` gate dropped.

```verilog
// shipped (correct):
assign exact_zero = eff_sub & (sum27 == 27'd0);
// B-M2 / D9 mutant:
assign exact_zero = (sum27 == 27'd0);
```

(A labeled comparison, not a listing: the first `assign` is quoted from the shipped `src/ch09/fp32_addsub.v`, the second from the scratch mutant build used throughout this chapter.)

The history, in one paragraph per chapter. Chapter 9 built the mutant and measured the masking: its unit bench kills B-M2 on one directed vector (the zero-operand effective ADD), and the composed `fp32_add2` then passes the entire 100,092-check golden sweep with the mutant inside — "Masking, Measured in Both Directions", the finding that made unit suites non-optional. Chapter 10 composed four inputs, threw its whole campaign at the mutant — 24,000 quadruples, 28 corner rows, the reachers — recorded total survival, and *proved the mask structural*: `exact_zero`'s entire fanout is one mux term in `fp32_round_pack.dp_res`; `dp_res` reaches the world only through `result = screen ? screen_res : dp_res`; and the two builds differ only when `eff_sub = 0` with `sum27 = 0`, which forces both operands to be zeros, which forces `screen = 1`. The gate protects a region of its input space that the screen never lets speak. Chapter 11 pipelined everything and measured the debt riding through unchanged — same modules, registers between them, same masked function per transaction — and its bridge set this chapter's obligation in plain words: *resolve it, or formally accept it in writing, with the structural argument attached.*

Before deciding anything, this chapter's research pass re-established the facts from scratch, and both reproduced this session: the unit kill —

```
FAIL tb_addsub_u es=0 big=000000 al=000000 grs=000: got 0000000/1 want 0000000/0
```

— and, with the same mutant composed into `fp32_add2`, the same masking —

```
PASS tb_equiv (92 corner checks + 100000 random, split == golden bit-for-bit incl. flags)
```

One framing matters more than anything else in this section, because the project's own running notes drifted into calling this "ch09's latent `exact_zero` bug", and the shorthand invites a misreading this chapter must correct: **the shipped module is correct against its contract, and the shipped composition is correct, full stop.** No test anywhere has ever caught the shipped design doing anything wrong here, and the next two sections — "The Fix That Isn't, and the Fix That Costs Three Chapters" and "The Acceptance: A Proof, Attacked" — prove none ever could. What rides into the flagship is not a wrong function. It is a *mutant class with no composition-level witness*: if anyone ever introduces B-M2 — a refactor, a rewrite, a copy of `fp32_addsub` into another project without its unit bench — no black-box test of `fp32_add2`, of either four-input structure, or of any pipeline of them can catch it. The debt is a hole in the verification's reach, not a defect in the design. Getting that distinction right is worth a chapter section, because the two readings demand opposite remedies: a bug wants a fix; an unwitnessable contract clause wants a proof and a witness. The guide's standing rule — write the weaker true statement — applies to debts too.

## The Fix That Isn't, and the Fix That Costs Three Chapters

The obvious disposition is "just fix `fp32_addsub`", and the reason this chapter does not take it is not taste — both candidate fixes were built and measured during the research pass, and the measurements are the argument.

**Route a1 — the minimal fix to `fp32_addsub` itself.** The only way to change the module is to ungate `exact_zero` (the mutant becomes the design) and move the sign decision into the consumer: `fp32_round_pack` gains an `eff_sub` input and packs `exact_zero ? {(eff_sub ? 1'b0 : sign), 31'd0} : ...`. Three measured results. First, the a1 build is bit-identical at the composition boundary — it passes the full 100,092-check golden sweep. Second, **it breaks the unit contract**: `tb_addsub_u` fails its zero-ADD vector against the a1 module, because the module's own specification changed — so "everything downstream stays green" is unsatisfiable by this route; chapter 9's unit bench would have to be rewritten to bless a new contract. Third, and decisively: **the latency does not die, it relocates.** Mutating the *relocated* gate — dropping the `eff_sub` term from the new pack mux — again passes the entire 100,092-check sweep. Same mask, new address. The finding generalizes: *some* logic in any correct implementation of S6 must decide the sign of a zero result; that logic's effective-add deciding case is "both operands zero"; and both-zero operands are screened. The latent region is a property of the **screen/datapath redundancy** — a don't-care region the screen creates — not of the file the gate happens to live in. No rewrite of `fp32_addsub` can remove it, only move it. The fix the debt's wording imagined does not exist.

**Route a2 — remove the redundancy instead: unscreen zeros.** If the latent region is the screen's shadow over zero operands, the root fix is to shrink the screen: delete `a_zero | b_zero` from the screen term and the zero rows from the screen mux (four lines in `fp32_screen.v`, nothing else). The shipped datapath already computes every zero case correctly — a zero operand unpacks to `sig = 0`, aligns to nothing, and adds as identity; both-zero effective adds pack `{sign_big, 31'd0}`; and the `exact_zero` gate must then *stay*, because ungated it would force `(−0)+(−0)` to +0. The research pass ran the a2 build through the complete downstream battery: chapter 9's six targets, chapter 10's three, chapter 11's eight — 17/17 green — plus chapter 10's 240,000-quadruple headline sweep at ×2 structures ×2 references, all bit-for-bit including flags against the *unchanged* golden references. So a2 computes exactly the shipped function, and it has a real payoff: **under a2, B-M2 dies in the ordinary suites** — this session, the a2-plus-mutant build fails `tb_equiv` on precisely the predicted vector:

```
FAIL tb_equiv 80000000+80000000: split 00000000/000 golden 80000000/000
```

`(−0)+(−0)` must be −0; the ungated mutant, with zeros now flowing through the datapath, forces +0 — four kills across two benches with no new tests written.

So why not a2? Because it is not a fix to a bug — there is no bug — it is a **re-decision of a taught design decision in three closed chapters**. `fp32_screen.v` is embedded byte-identical in chapter 9, whose prose teaches that the screen owns the signed-zero rule; chapter 10's D9 record and structural proof describe the zeros-as-specials design; chapter 11's bridge restates it. Adopting a2 means editing a shipped module and re-opening the prose of chapters 9, 10, and 11 plus a reviewed mutation record — all to close a verification hole that "The Acceptance: A Proof, Attacked" closes with seventeen vectors and zero design edits. The re-verification sweeps cost eight to twelve minutes of machine time depending on the day; the measurement is not the price. The price is churn in reviewed chapters and the loss of the textbook screen treatment the guide deliberately teaches. A verification debt should be paid with verification, and the shipped modules stay verbatim.

## The Acceptance: A Proof, Attacked

Accepting a design in writing means writing the acceptance down at the same standard as the design. Here is the document, in two parts: a proof, and an attack on the proof.

**Claim.** Over all 2^64 two-state operand pairs, the shipped `fp32_add2` and the B-M2-mutated `fp32_add2` compute identical `{result, invalid, overflow, inexact}`.

**Proof.** The two builds differ only in `exact_zero`, and only on inputs where `eff_sub = 0` and `sum27 = 0` — on every other input the two expressions agree. Suppose `eff_sub = 0` and `sum27 = 0`.

1. With `eff_sub = 0`, `sum27 = big27 + sml27` in 27 bits with **no wrap**: `big27 = {1'b0, sig_big, 2'b00} ≤ (2^24−1)·4 < 2^26` and `sml27 = {1'b0, aligned, g, r} < 2^26`, so the true sum is `< 2^27` and fits. Hence `sum27 = 0` iff `big27 = 0` **and** `sml27 = 0`.
2. `big27 = 0` iff `sig_big = 0`. `sig_big = {hidden, F}` of the operand with the larger packed magnitude, and `{hidden, F} = 0` iff `E = 0, F = 0` (chapter 7's `fp32_fields` contract: `hidden = (E != 0)`) — the bigger operand is a signed zero.
3. The swap invariant (`swap = b[30:0] > a[30:0]`, encoded-order compare) makes the big operand the one with the larger magnitude; if it is a zero, the other operand's magnitude is `≤ 0` — **both operands are zeros**.
4. Both operands zero implies `a_zero & b_zero`, so `screen = 1`.
5. `exact_zero`'s complete fanout is one mux term of `fp32_round_pack.dp_res`; it appears in neither `ovf` nor `inexact_dp` (three lines of shipped RTL). Under `screen = 1`: `result = screen_res`, independent of `dp_res`; `invalid = inv_scr`, never a function of `exact_zero`; `overflow = ~screen & ovf = 0`; `inexact = ~screen & inx_dp = 0`. Every output is independent of `exact_zero`. ∎

Each step stands on something already attacked: step 1 on chapter 8's "The Width Budget: 28 Bits" (assaulted three ways there and held), step 2 on chapter 7's classifier contract (67.1M-pattern sweep), step 3 on chapter 9's `fp32_swap` contract, step 5 on quoted RTL. Note what the proof never needs: it does not reason about alignment, sticky, or the small operand at all — step 1 forecloses the tempting loophole "a nonzero small operand shifted entirely out against a zero big operand", because a zero cannot be the *larger* magnitude of a pair containing a nonzero. And the composition lifts it unchanged: in the tree, every `fp32_addsub` sits inside a complete `fp32_add2` carrying its own screen, so the argument applies instance by instance to whatever operands arrive — driven or *born*, since a stage-1 exact cancellation delivers +0, a zero, to stage 2, where stage 2's own screen masks stage 2's own gate. In the pipelines the same modules compute the same function per transaction behind registers (chapter 11 measured exactly this). One honest caveat: this is a **two-state proof**. With X on the operands, `sum27 == 27'd0` can evaluate X and the builds could X-propagate differently — but no functional claim is made for X-contaminated operands in either build, and chapter 11's X-discipline (enforce at injection points) covers the pipe.

**The attack.** A proof about RTL can be right about the wrong RTL — the version its author remembered rather than the one that ships — so the research pass attacked it as if it were false, with generators aimed straight at its assumptions: equal magnitudes at the swap boundary, subnormal and ramp-edge operands, and above all zeros, driven and born. Method: build the shipped design and the B-M2 build, drive both with identical stimulus, print every `{operands, result, flags}` line, and diff the transcripts — any divergence anywhere becomes a diff line. Re-run in full this session:

- **Pair level** (seed 90210): 64 exhaustive pairs over {±0, ±min-subnormal, ±1.0, ±max-normal}, then 1,000,000 pairs at 40 % zero operands, 10 % subnormals, 10 % ramp-edge tiny normals, every fourth pair forced to exact cancellation `x, −x`. **1,000,064 pairs, transcripts byte-identical — 0 diff lines.**
- **Quad level** (seed 60606, through the tree): all 256 quads over {+0, −0, +1, −1} — every cancellation-born-zero pattern stage 2 can see — then 250,000 rounds × 4 attack patterns (`(x,−x,y,−y)` feeding stage 2 two *born* zeros as an effective add; born mixed with driven; zero-heavy random; three signed zeros plus one live value). **1,000,256 quads, transcripts byte-identical — 0 diff lines.**

Then the models re-verified the transcripts independently: `oracle.py` (spec-level, exact rationals) and `hwmodel.py` (algorithm-level events) each replayed the shipped transcripts — 2,000,320 vectors, results and flags, **zero mismatches** — closing the loop between the proof's subject, the shipped RTL, and the specification. Finally the pipeline was closed *empirically*, not just structurally: a B-M2 build of the entire shipped streaming target — mutant under both the flagship and its combinational oracle — passes all 60,992 streamed quads bit-for-bit. Chained with the quad-level diff (mutant-comb ≡ shipped-comb) and the assembly proof (shipped-pipe ≡ shipped-comb), that yields mutant-pipe ≡ shipped-pipe by three measured links, upgrading chapter 11's "rides through unchanged" from argument to measurement.

The search found no violation, and the proof says none exists to find. Both statements are needed. The proof is the claim over 2^64; the two million vectors are the check that the proof is about the RTL that actually ships.

## The Witness: Observing the Wire the Screen Cannot Mask

A proof plus an attack would still leave the mutant class unwitnessed — an acceptance paragraph standing in for a check, forever. It does not have to, because the proof itself names the instrument. The screen masks the datapath's *result*, not its *wires*: on a screened both-zero input the datapath still computes, `sum27` is still zero, and the gate's output is still there, observable hierarchically — chapter 10's T7/T8 discipline, aimed at last at the one place black-box checking measurably cannot reach. `tb_d9wit` drives all 16 signed-zero quadruples plus one cancellation-born-zero quadruple into the combinational tree and asserts the contract invariant **on the DUT's own wires**, per instance:

```verilog
      if (dut.u_add_ab.u_addsub.exact_zero === 1'b1 &&
          dut.u_add_ab.u_swap.eff_sub !== 1'b1) begin
        $display("FAIL tb_d9wit %h %h %h %h: u_add_ab exact_zero on an effective add", a, b, c, d);
        errors = errors + 1;
      end
```

(and identically for `u_add_cd` and `u_add_r`). Two guards make the check itself falsifiable, because an invariant is exactly the kind of check that can pass by never being tested — chapter 10's T7 lesson applied to an assertion rather than a bin. A **count guard**: 17 checks must run. An **arming counter**: the run fails unless at least one instance actually reached `eff_sub = 0` with `sum27 = 0` — unless the invariant was ever in a position to fail. The arming arithmetic is worth doing by hand, and python confirms it: among the 16 signed-zero quads, `u_add_ab` sees a same-signed (effective-add) zero pair in 8, `u_add_cd` in 8, and `u_add_r` — whose operands are the *born* level-1 zeros — in 10 (the 9 quads where neither level-1 pair is all-negative, plus the all-`−0` quad); the born-zero quad arms `u_add_r` once more. 8 + 8 + 10 + 1 = 27. Measured, shipped design:

```
PASS tb_d9wit (17 vectors, invariant armed 27 times across 3 instances)
```

And against the B-M2 build, this session: **27 FAIL lines** — one per armed instance-vector, the first on the all-`+0` quad at all three instances, the last on the born-zero quad at `u_add_r`, an instance armed purely by zeros the stimulus never drove:

```
FAIL tb_d9wit 42f6e979 c2f6e979 3f800000 bf800000: u_add_r exact_zero on an effective add
```

Chapter 10's one genuine mutation-record survivor is killable after all — by observing the wire the screen cannot mask. The witness then took its own medicine, per standing practice. Disabling the arming counter makes the run FAIL on the *correct* design (`invariant never armed`) — the precise guarantee is arming count ≥ 1: a witness that lost its stimulus entirely announces itself, while a partial stimulus loss degrades the kill count but still kills; deleting the check calls trips the count guard (`0 checks ran, expected 17`). And because a witness this important should not live only in one bench, the same invariant is folded into the coverage model as an armed **never-bin** — where it independently killed B-M2 this session (`exact_zero fired on an effective add 39 times`) while all 105 pinned counts still matched, a measured miniature of the whole D9 story: every screen-qualified bin is blind to the mutant, exactly as the proof requires, and only the deliberately unqualified never-bin sees it.

The disposition, stated once in the spec's language: *the four-input adder carries a module whose contract includes one clause no output can witness; this chapter proves the clause can never matter — a five-step proof attacked with 2,000,320 targeted vectors, zero divergence — and then witnesses it anyway, on the wire, in the shipped suite.* Measure, prove, guard; and know when not to change working hardware.

## The Coverage Model: 105 Bins on the DUT's Own Wires

Chapter 5's "Coverage, and Building One by Hand" closed with a seed addressed to this chapter by name: extend the 34-bin model with the missing *dimensions* — the sign combination whose absence let an all-positive campaign certify closure, and the rounding events — before any mechanical improvements. `tb_cov4` pays that seed in full: **105 bins**, all sampled from the pipelined DUT's own wires, every bin qualified in the stage where its transaction actually lives — chapter 11's rule from "Coverage Bins Travel With Their Transaction", now applied across three pipeline instances at two levels:

| Dimension | Bins | The seed it pays |
|---|---|---|
| operand class cross, per level-1 pair | 25 × 2 | ch05's cross, now at both tree positions |
| \|exponent difference\| buckets, per pair | 4 × 2 | ch05's buckets, boundaries straddled by the directed library |
| **sign / effective operation, per instance** | 2 × 3 | the ALL_POSITIVE blind spot, closed by name |
| **normalize partition** right-1 / none / left-N, per instance | 3 × 3 | ch08's shifter rows as white-box bins per stage |
| **rounding events** — guard set, sticky set, tie-up taken, tie-down taken, round-up, rounding-carry renormalize — per instance | 6 × 3 | ch05/ch08's rounding-event seed; the reachers get a bin at every stage |
| **intermediate events** — stage-1 overflow ×2, stage-1 NaN born ×2, stage-1 subnormal result ×2, inter-pair cancellation shallow/deep, stage-2 exact cancellation | 9 | ch10's composition events |
| result class at retire | 5 | ch05 |
| **the D9 never-bin**: `exact_zero` under `eff_sub` low, all three instances | (must be 0, must be armed) | the witness invariant as coverage |

Stage-correct qualification is not decoration; it is the difference between counting events and counting noise. Stage-1 bins (operand crosses, exponent distance, effective op) sample this cycle's inputs under `in_valid`, with the eff-op bins additionally qualified by `~screen` — an unqualified version counts screened NaN traffic as datapath arithmetic (mutation m7 measures it in the [add] bin: 51 counted where 18 happened). Stage-2 bins (normalize partition, rounding events) read each instance's *own* `vpipe[0]` and pipelined `p1_screen`, because the rounder's wires belong to the transaction one bank downstream of the ports. Retire bins read `out_valid`. The final adder's stage-1 bins qualify on `v_ab` — its transactions are the level-1 *results*, two cycles behind the ports. Get any of these wrong and the counters silently describe a different design; the next section, "Pinning the Bins: The Last Unpaid Debt", is about how you *catch* that.

The stimulus is a 58-quad deterministic directed library (generated by `cov_gen.py`, floor-closing all 105 bins by construction — chapter 5's corner-library principle at scale) followed by three 6,000-quad random regimes: chapter 5's biased menu, raw fields, and clustered exponents. 18,058 quads, 3.8 s, and the shipped run closes everything:

```
PASS tb_cov4 (18058 quads, 105/105 bins, pins matched, D9 never-bin armed 27124 and empty)
```

That PASS line contains the two guards "Pinning the Bins: The Last Unpaid Debt" explains — `pins matched` and the never-bin's arming count — and they, not the 105/105, are what make it worth printing.

## Pinning the Bins: The Last Unpaid Debt

Chapter 5 recorded a blind spot in its own coverage instrument and left it, deliberately, as the project's last unpaid verification debt: *"the bin definitions in `tb_covfp.v` are themselves unverified. Moving the `expdbin` sticky boundary from 25 to 60 still closes 34/34 silently."* A coverage model is a program that claims to describe your specification, and until this chapter nothing checked that claim. Here is the mechanism that does, and — because the mechanism is itself a testbench — the story of how it had to be debugged by mutation before it deserved trust.

**The guard.** The 58-quad directed phase is deterministic, so every bin's count over it is a *predictable number*. `cov_gen.py` implements **every bin definition a second time, independently, in python**, on top of `hwmodel.py` — the algorithm-level event model written from the documented algorithm rather than the RTL, and validated against both `oracle.py` and the RTL transcripts on over a million pairs this session. The generator runs the directed list through the python bins and emits the 105 expected counts as `cov_pins.vh`; after the directed phase drains, the bench compares **every counter against its pin with `!==`**, naming any bin that diverges. The consequence is a two-key lock: a bin definition edited in the bench alone breaks against python; edited in python alone, breaks against the bench; edited in both is a visible, reviewable spec change — the only kind the guard permits. On top of the pins sit partition invariants over the whole run (right1 + none + leftN must equal the stage-2 sample count per instance; eff-add + eff-sub the stage-1 count; tie-up + tie-dn the ties; renormalize ≤ round-up; result classes sum to retirements, retirements to drives), and the armed D9 never-bin.

**The battery.** Nine bin-definition mutations, each editing the coverage model — never the DUT; the correct design runs underneath every time. All nine were re-run this session against the shipped model: **9/9 killed.** The full table is in `src/ch12/README.md`; the flavor, in three rows. Chapter 5's exact recorded silent edit — `expdbin` boundary 25 → 60 — now dies in one line: `bin 52 expd_ab[d<25] = 7, python model pins 4`, because the library carries vectors at d = 24/25/26/27 straddling the boundary. Re-aiming the renormalize bin at the *wrong instance's* rounder — a real wire, elaborates clean, chapter 10's T7 class — dies as `bin 90 rev_r[round_renorm] = 2, pins 1`. And corrupting the **pin table itself** (one count changed by one) dies in the other direction: the guard is two-sided, so neither implementation can drift to meet the other.

**The two survivors that taught the design.** In the research pass's first round, two of the nine *survived*, and each survival was a genuine hole in the guard as first built — which is the section's real lesson, because a coverage guard is a testbench and gets the same discipline as one. Mutation m4 mistimed a stage-2 qualification (`vpipe[0]` → `vpipe[1]`) and passed, because the pinned phase was back-to-back traffic: under unbroken valids, a one-cycle-late qualifier samples the same stream shifted by one, losing only the *first* quad's events — and the library's first quad was all-zeros, with no stage-2 datapath events to lose. The fix was in the *library*, not the checker: make the first directed quad a live-datapath quad, and put bubbles inside the pinned phase (one every seven quads) so valid/invalid alternation distinguishes the stages. Chapter 11's rule, sharpened by a measured miss: **a stage-qualification bug is invisible to a pinned phase that never breaks the valid stream.** Mutation m5 moved the inter-pair-cancellation deep boundary from 8 to 16 and passed, because the library's cancel vectors sat at shifts 1 and 23 — both sides of *both* boundaries. A pinned count makes a boundary falsifiable only if the library **straddles it adjacently**; vectors at shift 7 and shift 8 were added and the mutation dies, moving both counters at once. Chapter 8's adjacency principle, promoted from result checking to bin-definition checking. The shipped library is the fixed one, and m4 and m5 die against it — their kill lines are in the record next to the seven that never survived. And the principle collected a third instalment after writing: this chapter's review widened the `d ≤ 2` exponent bucket to `d ≤ 3` and PASSED the whole shipped run, because the library had no vector between d = 3 and d = 24. The straddle vector (`4B000000 + 49800000`, d = 3) was added, the mutant now dies on two pins at once (m10 in the record), and the claim "boundaries straddled" is made by a library that has now been caught twice for the same reason.

That is the M18 debt paid, with interest: the payment mechanism was itself shown able to fail, twice, and repaired with directed vectors whose absence the mutations named.

## Closure, Measured Honestly

The number "105/105" is only worth what its failure modes cost, so the kit ships two deliberate failure runs alongside the green one, both compiled from the shipped `tb_cov4.v` with one `-D` flag.

**Random-only** (`-DNO_DIRECTED`): the three random regimes alone — 18,000 well-shaped quads, signs free — close **98/105 and fail with 7 named holes**: all three `round_renorm` bins, both stage-1 overflow bins, stage-2 exact cancellation, and result-class ZERO. The renormalize holes are chapter 8's "Round, and the Renormalize Random Never Finds" recurring at composition scale — 0 hits in 18,000 random quads across all three instances, exactly as it measured 0 in 1,000,000 pairs — and the rest are the same lesson: these are directed-only bins, and the hole list *names* them, which is the entire value of a bin over a hope.

**All-positive** (`-DALL_POSITIVE`): chapter 5's demonstration, re-run against the extended model at full scale. In chapter 5, the stimulus that never performed one effective subtraction closed the old 34-bin model 34/34 and was *certified* — the measured indictment that a model is silent about every dimension it does not name. Against this chapter's model the same stimulus closes **91/105 and fails with 14 named holes**: `effop[sub]` at all three instances, `norm[leftN]` at all three, both inter-pair-cancellation bins, stage-2 exact cancellation, result-class ZERO, all three `round_renorm` bins, and `s1_ovf_cd` — the dead subtract path spelled out bin by bin, on top of the directed-only holes. (One asymmetry is the generator's luck, worth reporting as measured: all-positive maxima did overflow the *ab* adder in this draw, so `s1_ovf_ab` is not among the fourteen.)

```
  HOLE: bin 59 effop_ab[sub]
  HOLE: bin 61 effop_cd[sub]
  HOLE: bin 63 effop_r[sub]
  ...
FAIL tb_cov4: 14 empty bins
```

That is the ch05 debt cashed: the blindness is now a detection. But state the conclusion at its honest strength, because the demonstration cuts both ways. Closure still measures stimulus *against the model* — nothing more. What changed is that the model itself is now falsifiable: its definitions are pinned to an independent implementation, its boundaries are straddled (twice repaired when mutation and review found unstraddled ones), its qualifications are mutation-tested, and its blind spots, where they exist, are at least not the ones any prior chapter has named. The honest closure statement for the shipped kit: *directed closes everything by construction; 18,000 well-shaped random quads close 98/105 and can never close the last seven; all-positive stimulus is detected instead of certified; and the number 105/105 means the stimulus reached everything a twice-implemented, ten-times-attacked model encodes.* A coverage percentage with that sentence under it is evidence. Without it, it is a mood.

The 101k runs remain two `-DNPR=20000` commands away and were re-run this session (38.1 s and 38.7 s, both PASS, quoted in "The Assembly Proofs").

## Seeds for Chapters 13 and 14

**For chapter 13 — what SystemVerilog would change.** Nearly everything in this chapter's kit is a hand-rolled version of something SystemVerilog names. The 105-bin array is a `covergroup` with `coverpoint`s and `cross`es; the pinned counts are `option.at_least` plus a checker the language does not provide at all — nothing in stock functional coverage verifies bin *definitions* against a second implementation, so the M18 guard remains your job in any language. The D9 witness is an `assert property` on a hierarchical path, and its arming counter is a `cover property` — the language pairs them for exactly the reason this chapter built both: an assertion that never triggers proves nothing without a cover showing its antecedent occurred. The streaming scoreboard is a `mailbox` and two `class`es in a UVM agent. None of it runs under Icarus — chapter 5 measured the support matrix ("Assertions, and What You Can Actually Run Here": no concurrent assertions, no covergroups at any `-g` level) — which is why every mechanism here exists in plain Verilog first. Chapter 13's job is the translation, with chapter 9's measured `always_comb` sensitivity finding as its opening exhibit; the concepts transfer because they were never tool features to begin with.

**For chapter 14 — the road not taken, with its price tag.** S3 and S10 exclude the single-rounding four-input sum, and this chapter measured what the exclusion costs: the composed answer misses the correctly rounded sum on 1.65 % of full-range quadruples and 28-32 % of clustered-exponent ones (this session's census), with the composed `inexact` over-reporting one-directionally on up to 7.095 % of clustered win2 quadruples whose bits land exactly right — and 0 % of full-range ones (chapter 10's table). Architectures that pay for the difference exist — fused multi-operand adders that keep the sum unrounded internally and round once, the same idea the FMA hardwired into IEEE 754-2008, and the research literature on correctly rounded reductions builds from there. That is chapter 14's frontier material: what it costs in alignment width and normalization depth to keep four operands exact until one rounding, who has built it, and how bfloat16's and posits' different precision economics change the trade. This chapter's contribution to that discussion is the honest baseline: a measured, specified statement of exactly how far the cheap composition is from the ideal, rather than a vague sense that "rounding error accumulates".

## Traps Beginners Fall Into

**Verifying against an unwritten specification.** The commonest failure in this chapter's territory is not a wrong test but a test with no defined right answer: "add the four numbers" is not a specification, as {max, max, −max, −max} demonstrates by returning three different defensible answers depending on decisions you did not know you were making. Write the clauses down first. Every ambiguity you leave unwritten becomes a bug report that is really a specification dispute — S1's port-order clause exists because chapter 10 measured exactly that dispute.

**Building the reference in the "natural" order.** A four-input reference model written as `((a+b)+c)+d` — the order a `for` loop produces — fails a correct tree DUT on 17.7 % of this suite's stream (mutation g1, measured this session). The reference follows the wiring, or it is not a reference; and the agreement of two structures with each other proves nothing about either (chapter 10's "'Both Orders Agree' Is Not an Oracle").

**Reading a coverage percentage as a quality statement.** 105/105 here means the stimulus reached everything *the model encodes* — under a model whose definitions are twice-implemented and mutation-tested. The same percentage from an unverified model meant nothing at all, as chapter 5's all-positive certification proved and this chapter's re-run turned into a 14-hole failure. Ask what the bins encode and who checks the bins before quoting the number.

**Trusting a checker you have never seen fail — including the meta-checkers.** This chapter's pin guard *itself* let two mutations survive until the library was fixed. The discipline is recursive: testbenches get mutation-tested, coverage models get mutation-tested, and the witness's arming guard exists because an invariant can pass forever by never being exercised. At every level the question is the same: what would have to break for this to go red?

**Treating a masked latent region as a bug — or as nothing.** Both readings of D9 are cheap and both are wrong. "It's a bug, fix it" purchased, when measured, a broken unit contract and a relocated mask (a1). "The proof says it can't matter, move on" leaves the mutant class unwitnessed forever. The measured disposition is proof *plus* witness — and the general lesson is that don't-care regions created by redundancy (a screen over a datapath here; clock gating over an ALU elsewhere) are where mutants go to hide, and hierarchical observation is the standing instrument for them.

**Sizing regression targets by their best-day runtime.** The same 101k-quad workload measured 36.7 s, 56.0 s, and 38 s across three sessions on the same machine. A target at 93 % of its timeout is a flake generator; recorded wall-clocks are observations, not contracts. Size to the slowest observed day, and raise the timeout for full-tree runs.
