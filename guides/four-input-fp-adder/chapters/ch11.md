## Three Adders, No Clock, and the Deal on the Table

Chapter 10 ended with three verified adders and a warning about time. `fp32_add2` is proven bit-identical to the golden algorithm over hundreds of thousands of checks; the tree and the chain compose it into four-input adders proven order-faithful over 240,000 quadruples. Every one of them is a combinational cloud: operands in, answer out, zero simulated time in between. And that is precisely their limit. In real hardware the answer is *not* instantaneous — `fp32_add2` is one long path of compare, a 26-position shift, a 27-bit subtract, a 26-input leading-zero count, another shift, an increment, and a pack, all in series — and a clock period must be long enough for the whole series to settle. A combinational adder does not fail when the clock is too fast for it; it *bounds the clock* of everything around it.

The remedy is the oldest deal in digital design: cut the long path with registers, and the clock only has to cover the longest *piece*. The price is paid in a new currency — **latency, counted in cycles**: the answer to the operands you present now arrives two or four clock edges later, while new operands enter every cycle behind them. This chapter takes that deal three times. `fp32_add2_p2` registers chapter 9's adder into two stages (latency 2), `fp32_add2_p4` into four (latency 4), and `fp32_add4_tree_p` pipelines chapter 10's tree by composing three of the 2-stage units (latency 4). Not one line of datapath logic is new anywhere: all three are chapter 9's seven modules, instantiated unmodified, plus register banks and wiring — the decomposition priced in "Cutting Along the Seams" is what makes this chapter cheap, and the manifest compiles chapter 9's exact files next to these to keep it honest.

Two things must be said before the first register is declared. First, chapter 10's bridge was right about the verification bill: the reference stays combinational while the DUT answers late, so the `#1`-and-check idiom is dead, the corner suite must become a *stream*, and every coverage bin needs re-timing. Most of this chapter is that bill, paid with measurements — a streaming-equivalence harness that ran over 900,000 transaction checks through the shipped designs and their mutants this session, and a mutation record in which **five distinct pipeline bug classes each pass a plausible weaker harness that this chapter also ships, as a labeled foil**. Second, the epistemic wall, stated up front and again where it bites: **no synthesis or static timing tool exists in this environment.** This chapter can prove, and does, that the pipelines compute bit-identical results to their combinational parents at one result per cycle; it can count registers, cycles, and kills. What it cannot do is name a nanosecond: delta cycles are not time, and the entire 101,111-pair streaming proof would pass unchanged if every stage were a thousand times deeper. Everything about physical timing lives in one clearly flagged section near the end, as documentation.

The working method is unchanged: Icarus Verilog 13.0, `iverilog -g2012 -Wall`, zero-output compiles on every target, every transcript below produced this session, every number re-verified in python3. Chapter 11 adds eight build targets, all green through `run_all.sh ch11` — including both streaming sweeps at full headline size, which no chapter before this could afford — and the full-repo regression stands at 103/103. And one measurement frames everything that follows: across every streamed check ever run in this chapter, pipelining changed **zero result bits**. The arithmetic, for the first time since chapter 6, does not change at all. What changes is *when* — and, as the mutants demonstrate, what can now go wrong is precisely the pairing of *when* with *what*.

## Cutting at the Priced Seams

Where do the registers go? Chapter 9 already answered, in bits. Its "Cutting Along the Seams" priced every module boundary from the shipped port lists, each figure including the 34-bit screen side channel (`screen`, `screen_res[31:0]`, `invalid`) that rides every cut: **100 bits** after unpack/screen/swap, **95** after align, **73** after addsub, **72** after normalize. A pipeline register bank is exactly one of those seams with flops on it, so the seam prices are the *register cost menu* for this chapter, and the two shipped depths are two purchases from it.

**`fp32_add2_p2` — two stages, latency 2.** Stage 1 is golden steps 1–5 (unpack, screen, swap, align, addsub); **bank 1 buys the 73-bit addsub cut** — the cheapest interior seam, and it happens to sit immediately after what is plausibly the deepest front-half logic, the 26-position alignment shift and the 27-bit subtract. Chapter 9 flagged that coincidence when it priced the seams; here it is cashed. Stage 2 is normalize, round/pack, and the screen mux; **bank 2 buys the 35-bit output cut**. Registered outputs are part of the design, not decoration: an unregistered final mux would hang the entire normalize/round cloud off the output port, re-exposing it to whatever combinational logic the consumer attaches. From `src/ch11/fp32_add2_p2.v`, the entire first purchase:

```verilog
  // ---- bank 1: the 73-bit addsub cut ----
  // 39 datapath bits + the 34-bit screen side channel. The screen is
  // computed ONCE, in stage 1; its RESULT is pipelined, never recomputed.
  reg        p1_sign_big, p1_eff_sub, p1_s_al, p1_exact_zero;
  reg [7:0]  p1_e_big;
  reg [26:0] p1_sum27;
  reg        p1_screen, p1_invalid;
  reg [31:0] p1_screen_res;
  always @(posedge clk) begin
    p1_sign_big   <= sign_big;
    p1_eff_sub    <= eff_sub;
    p1_s_al       <= s_al;
    p1_exact_zero <= exact_zero;
    p1_e_big      <= e_big;
    p1_sum27      <= sum27;
    p1_screen     <= screen;
    p1_invalid    <= inv_scr;
    p1_screen_res <= screen_res;
  end
```

That is what a pipeline stage *is*: a bank of non-blocking assignments capturing one seam's wires, nothing more. The rest of the file is chapter 9's instantiations with the stage-2 modules reading `p1_*` names instead of wires, and the back end is the second purchase plus the module's one genuinely new organ:

```verilog
  // ---- bank 2: the 35-bit output cut ----
  // Chapter 9's step-10 mux (result = screen ? screen_res : dp_res) moved
  // INTO this bank's non-blocking assignment: "a top module is wiring"
  // survives, with the wiring now clocked.
  reg [31:0] p2_result;
  reg        p2_invalid, p2_overflow, p2_inexact;
  always @(posedge clk) begin
    p2_result   <= p1_screen ? p1_screen_res : dp_res;
    p2_invalid  <= p1_invalid;
    p2_overflow <= ~p1_screen & ovf;
    p2_inexact  <= ~p1_screen & inx_dp;
  end

  assign result   = p2_result;
  assign invalid  = p2_invalid;
  assign overflow = p2_overflow;
  assign inexact  = p2_inexact;

  // ---- valid pipe: the only reset domain ----
  reg [1:0] vpipe;
  always @(posedge clk)
    if (!rst_n) vpipe <= 2'b00;
    else        vpipe <= {vpipe[0], in_valid};
  assign out_valid = vpipe[1];
```

The interface change is part of the specification: `clk`, `rst_n`, `in_valid` and `out_valid` join chapter 9's pure-function port list, and the valid bit is a one-bit shift register running beside the data — one flop per stage, no decoding, and — "Reset the Control, Not the Datapath" measures why — *the only thing in the design that is reset*.

**`fp32_add2_p4` — four stages, latency 4.** The same bank pattern at four seams: bank A at the 100-bit swap cut, bank B at 73, bank C at 72, bank D at 35, prefixes `a_`/`b_`/`c_`/`d_` and a 4-bit valid pipe (`src/ch11/fp32_add2_p4.v`). The one seam deliberately *not* registered is the 95-bit align cut — chapter 9's research notes predicted this verbatim when they kept align and addsub as separate modules — and the reasons stack: it is the most expensive interior seam on the menu, and fusing align+addsub into one stage keeps the G/R packing convention (`sml27 = {1'b0, aligned, g, r}`, the boundary that hosted chapter 9's only integration-visible bug) inside a single stage instead of stretching it across a register bank.

The bill, counted from the built banks rather than estimated: `fp32_add2_p2` holds 73 + 35 + 2 = **110 state bits**; `fp32_add2_p4` holds 100 + 73 + 72 + 35 + 4 = **284**. Depth 4 costs 2.6× the flops of depth 2 for the same function and the same one-result-per-cycle throughput — the concrete area-for-frequency trade that the timing section can only describe, sitting here as two integers. What depth 4 buys — a shorter worst stage, hence a faster legal clock — is exactly the quantity this environment cannot measure, and the chapter will not pretend otherwise.

## The Stage Bank: Chapter 3's Rule, Finally Measured

Chapter 3's "Blocking and Non-Blocking, Derived From the Queue" derived the rule — sequential logic uses `<=` — from the event queue, on toy examples. A pipeline is where violating it finally bites a real datapath, and the bite is much stranger than the folklore "blocking breaks pipelines." Two one-token mutants of `fp32_add2_p2` were built this session, each changing one bank's `<=` to `=`, and measured three ways.

**Mutant `blk1` (bank 1 blocking), under a focused probe.** Drive `b = 0` every cycle so each pair is screened and the result is `a`'s passthrough over two back-to-back register hops (`p1_screen_res → p2_result`), with `a` carrying the cycle index so every output names its transaction. Delivered latency, classified per cycle over 1,998 cycles:

```
blkprobe: lat2=999 lat1=999 other=0 runs=1998
first classifications: 2121212121212121212121212121212121212121212121212121212121212121
```

A strict **2,1,2,1 alternation**: on alternate cycles the transaction *skips a stage*. This is chapter 3's measured "Icarus alternates the order between time steps inside a single run" made visible as a pipeline whose latency flips every cycle — when bank 1's blocking writes happen to execute first, bank 2's same-edge read sees the freshly written values instead of last cycle's.

**The same mutant on ordinary arithmetic is invisible.** Re-run the probe with unscreened operands (finite `a` plus 1.0, the result read back through the normalize/round cloud): `lat2=1998 lat1=0 other=0` — every cycle at the correct latency, on the *broken* bank. The inference consistent with chapter 3's queue model — flagged as inference, since the scheduler's internals are not directly observable — is that the continuous assignments between the banks have not re-propagated by the time bank 2 samples on the same edge. What is certain is the measurement: **reg-to-comb-to-reg paths delivered latency 2 on every cycle; direct reg-to-reg paths alternated.**

**The full streaming harness** (built in "The Streaming-Equivalence Harness") integrates those two facts into a kill count: 407 mismatches in 101,111 — not the ~50,000 a naive "half the transactions corrupt" model predicts — 178 result-changed, 227 X-class, 1 flag-only, and 1 coverage-count violation, with the first kills at stream cycles 3, 5, 7, 9: the zero and sign corner pairs at the head of the library, delivered one cycle early over the reg-to-reg screen path, plus bubble-X adjacency leaking through the same hops. The entire 100,000-pair random phase of ordinary datapath arithmetic passed through the broken register bank without a single mismatch. Blocking in a stage bank is *conditionally, alternately, path-dependently* broken — worst exactly where tests look least (control side channels, X-adjacency), silent on the arithmetic a random campaign hammers.

**Mutant `blk2` (output bank blocking): PASS.** All 101,111 streamed checks, zero mismatches. Nothing inside the DUT reads bank 2 on the same edge, and the testbench samples at the negedge, so the half-cycle-early update has no observer anywhere in the system. Hold that thought: the four-input tree section composes this same mutant into a design where a downstream stage *does* consume it on the same clock edge — and measures a surprise about which readers a blocking-written register can and cannot corrupt, ending in the sharpest rule this guide can state about `<=`.

Neither mutant draws a peep from the tools: `iverilog -g2012 -Wall` compiles both with zero output, no runtime warning exists, and chapter 3's detection-table verdict ("What the Simulator Will and Will Not Tell You") stands in this design. The discipline is cheap; the failure mode of skipping it is a 0.4-per-cent-of-cycles heisenbug that passes 20,092 held-transaction checks — `tb_single` greenlights `blk1` in full — and hides inside a green streaming run of everything except zeros, signs, and bubbles.

## Reset the Control, Not the Datapath

Chapter 3's "Reset Strategy" taught the mechanics; the pipeline forces the *policy* question: which of the 110 (or 284) state bits deserve a reset? The shipped answer — only the valid pipe — looks stingy until you measure the alternatives. `src/ch11/tb_reset.v` runs all three strategies from cold start in one simulation, on three instances of the same design, at both depths (the manifest runs it twice via `stream_cfg_p4.v`). Row A is the shipped design used as shipped; row B ties `rst_n` high and never resets anything; row C holds *every datapath register* at zero through reset — a hierarchical deposit, so no second design is needed, with the same post-release state a reset fanout to every flop would produce. The 2-stage transcript, first three lines (the remaining rows repeat the steady state):

```
tb_reset row B obs 0: out_valid=x (never-reset valid pipe)
tb_reset obs 0: A ov=0 r=xxxxxxxx f=xxx | C ov=0 r=7f800000 f=011
tb_reset obs 1: A ov=1 r=40400000 f=000 | C ov=1 r=40400000 f=000
```

**Row C is the section's centerpiece, and it is a trap.** "Reset everything to zero" *manufactures* `7F800000` with overflow and inexact raised — a fully defined **+infinity that no operand pair ever produced** — on the very first post-release cycle. The mechanism is worth tracing because it generalizes: all-zeros is an *illegal* datapath state. `e_big = 0` cannot occur in live traffic (the effective exponent is `max(E,1)` by chapter 7's contract), and stage 2 faithfully decodes the impossible: `e_lim = 0 − 1` wraps to 255, the leading-zero count of an all-zero frame is 26, the exponent arithmetic lands on `e_norm = 0 − 26 = 486` in 9-bit arithmetic, `486 > 254` fires the overflow path, and out comes +inf/overflow/inexact — every step verified in python against the shipped `fp32_normalize`/`fp32_round_pack` source. On the 4-stage version the fill window is worse in a more instructive way: `00000000` with clean flags (defined-looking +0), then the manufactured +inf **twice**, as the wrapped state marches down the banks. Full reset does not make the fill window safe; it replaces honest X with *confident-looking nonsense* — chapter 9's "X-Propagation: The Wrong Answer That Looks Right" lesson in a new costume, where THE RESET VALUE ITSELF is the X that looks fine. (A real reset net to all ~110/284 flops would also cost fanout and routing — that cost claim is documentation, no synthesizer here; the functional claim is the transcript above.)

**Row B measures why the valid pipe, and only the valid pipe, needs the reset.** With nothing reset, `out_valid` is `x` on the first observable cycle. A consumer's `if (out_valid)` skips the garbage only because Verilog evaluates `x` as false — luck, not design: `if (out_valid !== 0)`, or `out_valid` handed to arithmetic or to an interrupt line, takes the garbage. One flop per stage of reset buys a hard, checkable `0`.

**Row A is the shipped pattern.** `out_valid` is a hard 0 through the whole fill window — the first operands enter on the release edge itself, so LAT capturing edges elapse and the first valid-flagged output lands at observation LAT−1; fill-window data is X-contaminated garbage (asserted — `tb_reset` *requires* an x in row A's fill data, and requires row C's to be fully defined, so both halves of the lesson are regression-guarded); and that first valid-flagged output is already bit-correct — measured at both depths, no partially-flushed transition cycle was ever observed. The data banks need no reset because **gating, not data cleanliness, is what makes fill outputs ignorable**. That is the rule to carry to chapter 12: reset the control, gate by valid, and if some later design must reset data anyway, reset it to a *legal* don't-care — never to the pretty, illegal zero.

## The Streaming-Equivalence Harness

Chapter 9's central verification move was the golden-equivalence sweep ("Composition, and the Golden-Equivalence Sweep"); this chapter's is the same sweep **under continuous streaming**: a NEW operand pair every cycle, results compared LAT cycles later. "Skew, and the Other Four: Bugs That Pass a Weaker Harness" measures why the streaming is not optional; this section builds the machine, every piece of which earned its place.

**The oracle is chapter 9's combinational `fp32_add2` itself, instantiated in the same simulation.** The pipelined DUT's specification is "bit-identical to chapter 9's adder, LAT cycles late" — so the reference *is* that adder, fed the same operands in the same slot, and the equivalence chain back through chapter 8's golden model to exact arithmetic is inherited rather than re-proven. One harness source (`src/ch11/tb_stream.v`) compiles against both depths; `stream_cfg_p4.v` listed ahead of it in the manifest retargets `` `DUT``/`` `LAT`` at the 4-stage pipeline. The heart is one task, called once per stream slot:

```verilog
  // One stream slot. Check-before-overwrite is the load-bearing ordering,
  // and ONE index serves both.
  task slot(input valid, input [31:0] wa, input [31:0] wb);
    begin
      @(negedge clk);
      idx = cyc % LAT;                    // ONE index for check AND store
      if (cyc >= LAT) begin
        if (vq[idx]) begin
          if (ov !== 1'b1) begin
            mismatches = mismatches + 1;  // valid slot must retire valid
            if (mismatches <= 10)
              $display("FAIL tb_stream cyc %0d: %h+%h retired out_valid=%b, expected 1",
                       cyc, aq[idx], bq[idx], ov);
          end else if ((^{r_p, inv_p, ovf_p, inx_p}) === 1'bx) begin
            xfails = xfails + 1; mismatches = mismatches + 1;
            if (mismatches <= 10)
              $display("FAIL tb_stream cyc %0d: %h+%h X in outputs: %h/%b%b%b",
                       cyc, aq[idx], bq[idx], r_p, inv_p, ovf_p, inx_p);
          end else if ({r_p, inv_p, ovf_p, inx_p} !== eq[idx]) begin
            mismatches = mismatches + 1;
            if (r_p !== eq[idx][34:3]) mm_res = mm_res + 1;
            else                       mm_flag = mm_flag + 1;
            if (mismatches <= 10)
              $display("FAIL tb_stream cyc %0d: %h+%h pipe %h/%b%b%b ref %h/%b%b%b",
                       cyc, aq[idx], bq[idx], r_p, inv_p, ovf_p, inx_p,
                       eq[idx][34:3], eq[idx][2], eq[idx][1], eq[idx][0]);
          end
          n_checked_valid = n_checked_valid + 1;
        end else begin
          if (ov !== 1'b0) begin          // bubble stays bubble
            mismatches = mismatches + 1;
            if (mismatches <= 10)
              $display("FAIL tb_stream cyc %0d: bubble retired out_valid=%b, expected 0",
                       cyc, ov);
          end
          n_checked_bubble = n_checked_bubble + 1;
        end
      end else begin
        if (ov !== 1'b0) begin            // hard 0 before fill
          mismatches = mismatches + 1;
          if (mismatches <= 10)
            $display("FAIL tb_stream cyc %0d: out_valid=%b during fill, expected 0",
                     cyc, ov);
        end
      end
      in_valid = valid;
      a = wa; b = wb;
      #1;                                 // combinational reference settles
      vq[idx] = valid;
      aq[idx] = wa; bq[idx] = wb;
      if (valid) begin
        eq[idx] = {r_c, inv_c, ovf_c, inx_c};
        n_valid_driven = n_valid_driven + 1;
      end
      cyc = cyc + 1;
    end
  endtask
```

Read it as four disciplines stacked. **The scoreboard** is a LAT-deep circular queue, one slot per cycle: check the output against the expectation stored LAT cycles ago, *then* overwrite that slot with this cycle's — and one shared index computation serves both. Mutation-measured, both ways: shifting the shared index (`(cyc+1) % LAT` everywhere) is a **no-op** — all 101,111 checks still pass, because a circular queue read and written through one index is invariant under constant rotation — while computing the *read* index separately fails the fully CORRECT pipeline at stream cycle 3, the first adjacent differing pair. Two lessons from one pair of runs: the shared-index shape makes chapter 5's scoreboard off-by-one structurally impossible to express, and a scoreboard skew bug announces itself *against correct hardware* rather than silently blessing broken hardware — provided results actually differ cycle to cycle, which the back-to-back corner stream guarantees from its third slot. **Bubbles are first-class and adversarial**: a bubble slot drives all-X operands on purpose, must retire `out_valid === 0`, and `out_valid` is `===`-checked on *every* cycle including the fill — that per-cycle valid check *is* the latency measurement, not a separate test. **Data is `!==` behind an X-guard**, chapter 9's rule carried forward and re-proven here in streaming form: weakening to `!=` and deleting the guard produced a full-length false PASS against a pipeline whose sticky register is never driven (the record's T2), while the shipped harness kills that same design 61,486 times starting at cycle 12. **Count guards close the vacuity holes** chapter 5's "The Green Run That Means Nothing" catalogued: checked-valid must equal driven-valid (a truncated drain fails — measured), driven-valid must reach a literal floor (compiling with `-DNPR=0` dies with `only 1114 valid pairs driven`), and the `out_valid` cycle count must equal the driven count — which is the throughput measurement, banked as an equality.

Stimulus, all back-to-back at one pair per cycle: chapter 8's 46-pair corner library in both orders — 92 slots with **no idle cycles**, which silently upgrades the library itself into an adjacency test — then ten directed adversarial-adjacency slots, a bubble flush, the five-regime random generator at 100,000 pairs, 2,000 slots of random valid/bubble mix with X data on every bubble, and a drain under X. Seeded once, first draw discarded, seed echoed — chapter 5's rule, unchanged. The verdicts, verbatim from this session:

```
PASS tb_stream LAT=2 (101111 valid pairs incl 92 corners + 10 adjacency, 998 bubbles, 1 result/cycle, renorm corner=12 random=0)
PASS tb_stream LAT=4 (101111 valid pairs incl 92 corners + 10 adjacency, 1000 bubbles, 1 result/cycle, renorm corner=12 random=0)
```

Zero mismatches, zero X-guard hits, out_valid count equal to driven count, at both depths, ~10 seconds each — which is why, unlike chapter 10's out-of-harness 240k headline, these sweeps ship in the manifest at full size. The headline evidence of this chapter re-proves itself on every regression run.

## Skew, and the Other Four: Bugs That Pass a Weaker Harness

The characteristic NEW bug class a pipeline introduces is not wrong arithmetic. It is **skew**: one signal crosses a cut without its register, so a stage mixes two transactions. The mutant is a one-token diff of `fp32_add2_p2` — `u_norm`'s `s_in` wired to the stage-1 combinational `s_al` instead of the registered `p1_s_al`. It is the single easiest register to forget: sticky is one bit, it travels "around" the adder rather than through it, the design compiles with zero output, and the pipeline works everywhere else.

Two harnesses met this mutant this session. Both use the same corner library, the same five-regime generator, the same `!==`+X-guard+count-guard discipline. The ONLY difference is the schedule.

**`tb_single` — the shape a beginner writes first** (drive one pair with a one-cycle valid pulse, HOLD the operands, wait for `out_valid`, check, idle, next): **PASS, 20,092 of 20,092 checks — on the broken design.** Holding the operands makes this transaction's `s_al` wire and the previous transaction's `p1_s_al` register carry the same value at every check; the bug is not merely missed, it is *unexpressible* under this schedule. The file ships in `src/ch11/` anyway, as a labeled foil — it is honest, guarded, and able to fail (the undriven-register mutant kills it 12,299 times), which makes its green run on the skew mutant the most instructive transcript in the chapter.

**`tb_stream` — a new pair every cycle: 5,310 mismatches in 101,111** (5.25 %), classified by the harness itself: 3,909 flag-only, 1,152 result-changed (rounding flipped by a neighbor's sticky), 249 X-guard hits. The first kill lands at stream cycle 13, *inside the corner phase*:

```
FAIL tb_stream cyc 13: bfc00000+3fc00000 pipe 00000000/001 ref 00000000/000
FAIL tb_stream cyc 15: 3f800000+00000001 pipe 3f800000/000 ref 3f800000/001
FAIL tb_stream cyc 103: 00800000+80000001 X in outputs: 007fffff/00x
```

Read those three lines slowly; they are the whole bug class. At cycle 13 the exact-cancellation pair retires with `inexact=1` **stolen from its neighbor** `00000001+3F800000`, whose alignment sticky is live. Two cycles later the mirror image: the neighbor retires with its own sticky *lost*. And at cycle 103 a bubble slot's deliberate all-X operands leak through the unregistered path into a *valid* neighbor's outputs — inter-transaction X contamination, measured. The corner library kills this mutant at cycle 13 *when streamed back-to-back*; the same library applied one-held-pair-at-a-time proves nothing about skew. Streaming is to pipelines what chapter 9's integration sweep was to module seams: the only level of testing that observes the composition property itself — here, the pairing of data with transaction.

The record generalizes. Five distinct pipeline-specific bug classes were built and measured this session, and **every one passes a plausible weaker harness** while the streaming discipline kills it:

| Bug class (mutant) | Passes | Killed by | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data skew (`s_al` unregistered) | `tb_single`, 20,092 checks | `tb_stream`: 5,310 (5.25 %) | first kill cyc 13; flags stolen/lost between neighbors; bubble-X leaks |
| Flag mis-alignment (tree, flag delay omitted) | `tb_single4`, 5,016 held quads | `tb_stream4`: 16,162 (16.0 %) | **every data word correct**; flag-only + X |
| Blocking stage bank (`blk1`) | `tb_single`; and 100k random *within* the stream | `tb_stream`: 407 | 2,1,2,1 latency alternation on reg-to-reg paths only |
| Illegal reset state (full datapath reset) | `tb_stream`, all 101,111 — valid-gating masks it | `tb_reset` row C | defined +inf/overflow manufactured during fill |
| Valid/data tearing (same-edge driving) | a wait-for-valid harness — self-consistently late | `tb_stream`'s fill-cycle `===` checks | `out_valid=1` certifying `xxxxxxxx` (measured below) |

The fourth and fifth rows deserve their asterisks spelled out. The full-reset mutant *survives* the streaming harness — legitimately, because under valid-gated consumption it computes the same function after fill; its bug lives in what an *ungated* observer sees, so its witness is the cycle-pinned cold-start check of `tb_reset`, not an equivalence sweep. And valid/data tearing is a *testbench*-inflicted bug, measured in "Throughput, Latency, Fill, Drain — Counted" below: it needs the latency check pinned to the specification, which is exactly what the per-cycle `===` on `out_valid` does. One harness shape covers three of the five outright and defines the spec-anchors that catch the other two. That asymmetry — not any single kill — is this chapter's thesis.

## Coverage Bins Travel With Their Transaction

Chapter 9's regression counts a `round_renorm` coverage bin — the rounding-carry renormalize that chapter 8's "Round, and the Renormalize Random Never Finds" measured firing zero times in a million random pairs — qualified by `!screen` and proven non-empty by four directed reachers. Its notes predicted "the coverage bins will need re-timing when the boundaries acquire registers." Measured here, that re-timing has **two separate parts**, and each was gotten wrong in a recorded mutation before the right number appeared. The shipped counter, from `src/ch11/tb_stream.v`:

```verilog
  always @(posedge clk)
    if (dut.vpipe[LAT-2] === 1'b1 && dut.`RSCREEN === 1'b0 &&
        dut.u_round.round_renorm === 1'b1)
      rr_count = rr_count + 1;
```

with `` `RSCREEN`` naming the *pipelined copy* of screen at the round stage — `p1_screen` on the 2-stage design, `c_screen` on the 4-stage — and the count pinned to an expected `=== 12` over the corner+adjacency stream: the four reachers × both orders from the corner library, plus the four single-order reachers streamed inside the adjacency block.

1. **Validity qualification.** Drop the screen qualifier entirely and the counter reads **14**, not 12. The two extras are `7F800000 + 7F7FFFFF` in both orders — a SCREENED case whose don't-care datapath garbage happens to fire the second renormalize. Chapter 9's `!screen` qualifier is not bookkeeping; without it, garbage inflates coverage.
2. **Stage-correct qualification.** Re-use the qualifier naively as the top-level `screen` *wire* — the input-side, stage-1 signal, one to three transactions younger than the one in the round stage — and the counter reads **9** on the 2-stage pipe and **11** on the 4-stage: wrong in a depth-dependent way, because each firing is being gated by some *other* transaction's screen bit.

The general rule this measures: **when a datapath acquires stages, every coverage qualifier must travel to the stage where its bin samples.** A bin qualified by an input-side signal in a pipelined design is counting a property of one transaction against the identity of another — and the error is invisible without an expected count, because 9, 11 and 14 are all plausible-looking nonzero values that would sail through chapter 9's `!= 0` empty-bin gate (chapter 5's "Coverage, and Building One by Hand" warned that empty-bin gates are the floor, not the ceiling). The directed `=== 12` is what caught every wrong variant here, and it caught something else unplanned: the *blocking-bank* mutant `blk1` retires a screened corner transaction one cycle early, and the stage-qualified counter duly read 13 — the re-timed bin is itself a mutation detector.

Two standing facts re-confirmed under streaming, for chapter 12's regression file: `renorm random=0` in both PASS lines — 100,000 five-regime random pairs plus the mixed phase fired the rounding-carry renormalize **zero** times, consistent with chapter 8's 0-in-1M measurement, so the directed reachers remain the only source and must be carried, streamed, and counted at the right stage; and the count is stable across depths *only* when stage-qualified, which is the portable form of the rule.

## Throughput, Latency, Fill, Drain — Counted

Cycles are the one unit of time this environment *can* measure, so every cycle-denominated property of the shipped pipelines was counted rather than asserted.

**Throughput = 1 result per cycle after fill, as an equality.** A free-running counter increments on every cycle with `out_valid === 1`; at end of run it must *equal* the number of valid pairs driven. `101,111 === 101,111` at both depths this session, with the 100,000 random pairs driven on 100,000 consecutive cycles. The total simulated cycle count (~102,1xx) exceeds one-per-pair by exactly the fill, the deliberate bubble stretches, and the drain — nothing else.

**Latency = stage count, measured three independent ways.** (a) The streaming harness checks `out_valid === 0` on every fill cycle and `out_valid === vq[slot]` on every cycle after — a one-cycle latency error anywhere in ~102k cycles fails, which is precisely how the `-DLAT=3` harness mutation dies at cycle 2. (b) The one-pulse probe — a single valid transaction into an idle pipe:

```
cyc 2: out_valid=1 result=40400000 flags=000
PASS tb_pulse LAT=2: exactly one out_valid, at cycle 2, result 40400000
```

and the same at cycle 4 on the 4-stage design: **exactly one** `out_valid` cycle, at cycle LAT, carrying the correct result (`3F800000+40000000 → 40400000`: 1.0 + 2.0 = 3.0). (c) The cold-start rows of `tb_reset`: first valid-flagged output at observation LAT−1 from the release-and-drive edge, at both depths, already bit-correct.

**Fill and drain are where a pipeline's bookkeeping either exists or doesn't.** Fill: LAT cycles of hard-0 `out_valid` from reset release, then results — no partially-processed transition cycle was ever observed. Drain: the stream ends with `in_valid` low and **all-X operands** while the last LAT transactions retire, and every one retired bit-correct — measured, not assumed, because the drain Xs would poison any unregistered path (that is exactly how the skew mutant's 249 X-class kills arose). The banks fully isolate in-flight transactions from the input bus. Bubbles: ~1,000 random mid-stream bubbles with X data retired as `out_valid === 0` exactly LAT cycles later, every neighboring valid slot bit-correct; the valid-bit shift register is all the flow control a no-stall pipeline needs, and nothing else in the design knows bubbles exist.

**Driving on the wrong edge tears valid from data.** Chapter 4's "Clock and Reset Without Racing Your Own Design" gave the negedge rule; the pipeline gives it teeth. Re-driving the streaming probe on the *posedge* — the DUT's own sampling edge — produced not corruption but a clean, deterministic **latency of 3** (1,997/1,997 cycles, identical for blocking and non-blocking drive styles): the pipe is simply one cycle longer than designed, and a wait-for-out_valid harness would pass it with the wrong latency, everything self-consistently late. That is why `tb_stream` pins latency to the *specification* (fill-cycle counts), never to the DUT's own behavior. The one-pulse version of the same mistake is louder, and it is the section's key transcript:

```
cyc 2: out_valid=1 result=xxxxxxxx flags=xxx
cyc 3: out_valid=1 result=40400000 flags=000
```

`out_valid` fires **twice** — first certifying `xxxxxxxx` as a valid result, then delivering the real one. The mechanism: `in_valid` is read *directly* by the valid pipe, so the same-edge race resolves it a cycle earlier than the data, which lags through stage-1 logic — control and data take different-depth paths to their first registers, and same-edge driving un-pairs them. Nothing "raced" in the folklore sense anywhere in these runs; what same-edge driving measurably did is **tear valid from data**. The negedge rule is not superstition; it is what keeps control and payload in the same slot.

A sim-economy note for regression planning: the full 101,111-pair streaming run costs ~10.0–10.2 s per depth here, and `tb_single`'s 20,092 held transactions cost 2.1 s — about 10k versus 9.5k checks per wall-clock second, essentially the *same price per check*. Streaming's advantage is coverage class, not simulation economy: it sees skew at no extra cost.

## X in the Pipe, Quantified

Chapter 9 taught that X-propagation does not advertise itself; the pipeline lets that lesson be *counted*. `src/ch11/tb_xinj.v` streams 20,000 within-30-exponent pairs — the regime where the alignment sticky is most often live — and every 16th transaction hierarchically deposits `x` into the registered sticky `p1_s_al` for exactly one stage-2 evaluation (the next bank capture overwrites it). Clean slots are checked against the combinational reference throughout; injected slots are classified at retirement. The verdict line, this session:

```
PASS tb_xinj: injected=1250 absorbed_correct=767 absorbed_WRONG=0 visible_x=483 clean_mismatch=0
```

**61.4 % of single-bit X events on the registered sticky were absorbed into fully defined, bit-CORRECT outputs.** The X lands where round-to-nearest-even had already decided — `round_up = ng & (nr | ns | lbit)` did not need the sticky — and vanishes without trace. 38.6 % reached an output bit as visible x. Zero produced a defined-but-wrong output through this bit. (The split is seed- and regime-dependent — the research pass measured 59/41 with a different seed and probe — but the majority-absorbed structure is what reproduces, and the shipped bench fails on a degenerate split, so the claim stays regression-guarded.)

Three consequences, stated at measurement strength. First, **an output X-guard is a real but PARTIAL X detector** — structurally blind to the absorbed majority. Here the absorbed events were harmless; the blindness is not a guarantee that they always are, and that asymmetry is the point: X-discipline must be enforced at *injection points* — reset strategy, valid gating, drain isolation, the places this chapter's design decisions live — not detected at outputs after the fact. Second, the mutation T7 measured the guard's counterfactual: depositing a *defined wrong* sticky (`1'b0`) instead of `x` immediately produced fully defined, wrong results (`ed7e62ed/000` on the first injection) — X-pessimism was the only thing making the corruption visible, and a defined wrong value sails. Third, chapter 9's "X-Propagation: The Wrong Answer That Looks Right" class — the sign-bit X that yields a defined wrong result with clean flags — was *not* reproduced through this control bit: X-pessimism kept every influential sticky-X visibly x. That finding lives in comparison/mux-select logic (the swap's magnitude compare), which in the pipelined design still sits in stage-1 combinational logic; registering the seams neither fixed it nor worsened it. Pipelining relocates X risk to the state boundaries — where reset policy and valid gating are the controls — and this section plus the reset measurements are chapter 12's checklist for both.

## Latency as Horizontal Distance: A VCD, Parsed

Chapter 4 planted a seed when it read a two-stage toy in a waveform: *pipeline latency is horizontal distance — count the rising edges between a value on the input and the same value on the output*. This chapter cashes that seed with a measurement on the real design, and the measurement includes a trap.

`src/ch11/tb_wave.v` runs both pipeline depths side by side on one bus: a quiet carrier (1.0 + 1.0 = 2.0 every cycle) with ONE marker pair (π + π = 2π) embedded at slot 10. The testbench itself asserts the marker's retirements — `40C90FDB` exactly once on each output, at slots 12 and 14 — and dumps a VCD only behind chapter 4's `+dump=` plusarg convention, so nothing is ever written into the source tree. `$dumpvars(0, tb_wave)` on both full hierarchies costs ~32 KB for a 26-cycle run (the exact byte count varies with the `+dump=` path, which is itself stored in the file): full-design dumping of this pipeline is cheap. The dump was then parsed with the same python discipline chapter 4 used — never guess a signal name; read the `$var` table:

```python
# Chapter 11: measure pipeline latency from the VCD tb_wave.v dumps.
# Never guess signal names -- parse the $var table (chapter 4's aliasing
# finding: the same net can carry different ids in different scopes).
import sys

ids = {}                 # id -> full hierarchical name (first definition)
scope = []
vals = {}                # id -> current value string
first = {}               # (name, value) -> first time seen
t = 0
posedges = []            # times of clk rising edges

for line in open(sys.argv[1]):
    w = line.split()
    if not w:
        continue
    if w[0] == "$scope":
        scope.append(w[2])
    elif w[0] == "$upscope":
        scope.pop()
    elif w[0] == "$var":
        name = ".".join(scope + [w[4]])
        ids.setdefault(w[3], name)
    elif w[0].startswith("#"):
        t = int(w[0][1:])
    elif w[0].startswith("b"):           # vector change: bVALUE id
        name = ids.get(w[1], "?")
        v = w[0][1:]
        if "x" not in v:
            key = (name, "%08x" % int(v, 2))
            first.setdefault(key, t)
    elif w[0][0] in "01xz":              # scalar change: 0id / 1id
        name = ids.get(w[0][1:], "?")
        if name.endswith("clk") and w[0][0] == "1" and vals.get(name) == "0":
            posedges.append(t)
        vals[name] = w[0][0]

t_pi  = first[("tb_wave.a",    "40490fdb")]      # marker enters
t_2p2 = first[("tb_wave.r_p2", "40c90fdb")]      # 2-stage result
t_2p4 = first[("tb_wave.r_p4", "40c90fdb")]      # 4-stage result
for nm, te in [("r_p2", t_2p2), ("r_p4", t_2p4)]:
    lat = sum(1 for p in posedges if t_pi < p <= te)
    print("a->pi at #%d  %s->2pi at #%d  delta = %.0f ns, capturing posedges = %d"
          % (t_pi, nm, te, (te - t_pi) / 1000.0, lat))
```

Output, this session:

```
a->pi at #130000  r_p2->2pi at #145000  delta = 15 ns, capturing posedges = 2
a->pi at #130000  r_p4->2pi at #165000  delta = 35 ns, capturing posedges = 4
```

**The honest number is LAT·T − T/2, not LAT·T.** With a 10 ns clock, the 2-stage delta reads 15 ns, not 20; the 4-stage 35, not 40 — because the input edge sits on the *drive* negedge and the result edge on a *capture* posedge, so raw time subtraction under-reads the latency by half a period. Anyone eyeballing cursor deltas in a viewer will hit this. What is exactly LAT is the count of **capturing posedges strictly after the input edge, up to and including the result edge**: 2 and 4, machine-counted above. Read latency by counting clock rises between the two changes, never by subtracting cursor positions.

Two chapter-4 findings resurfaced in this dump, now on the FP design itself. The **partial-aliasing trap** is live: `clk` shares one identifier across the testbench and both DUT scopes, but `tb_wave.r_p2` and `dut2.result` — the same net — carry *different* identifiers, so a parser keyed on one name silently misses the other's changes (that exact mistake cost one debug round in the research pass; the listing's `$var`-table discipline is the cure). And parameters (`MARK`, `NSLOT`) are dumped, while `fp32_normalize`'s `lzc26` function appears as a `$scope function` — the hierarchy in the file is richer than the module tree. What cannot be shown here, unchanged since chapter 4's "Viewing Waveforms in 2026": no batch waveform renderer exists in this environment, so the viewer picture — the marker visibly sliding two and four columns right on the two result rows — is documentation; the parsed times above are the measurement.

## Pipelining the Four-Input Tree

Chapter 10 chose the tree for chapter 12 partly on a forecast: two levels pipeline into 2S stages with no balancing registers. This section builds it. `src/ch11/fp32_add4_tree_p.v` is three `fp32_add2_p2` instances in chapter 10's exact tree shape — `(a+b)+(c+d)`, port mapping pinned — with the valid bit chained level to level (`u_add_r.in_valid = u_add_ab.out_valid`): 2 levels × latency 2 = **latency 4**, one quad per cycle, zero new datapath logic. Its streaming harness `tb_stream4.v` keeps the same scoreboard shape with the oracle now chapter 10's *combinational* `fp32_add4_tree` in the same simulation, 16 directed quads (the `{max,max,−max,−max}` multiset in both port arrangements, L1-overflow-then-inf-absorption, sNaN, qNaN payload, subnormal cancellation, signed zeros, renormalize reachers in BOTH level-1 adders at once — flaggy quads deliberately adjacent to benign ones), 100,000 five-regime random quads, the bubble mix, the X drain:

```
PASS tb_stream4 (101019 valid quads incl 16 directed, 1000 bubbles, 1 result/cycle, latency 4)
```

Bit-identical including all three flags, in 36.7 s — inside the harness timeout, so the tree's headline also ships at full size.

**The composition's one genuinely new design obligation is flag alignment.** Chapter 10's "Flags Under Composition" made the flag rule a combinational OR across the three operations. In the pipeline, level-1 flags for quad *n* retire at *n*+2 while the level-2 result retires at *n*+4 — so the level-1 OR must ride a 2-cycle delay line to meet its own transaction. From the shipped file, this is *all* the new logic four-input pipelining needs:

```verilog
  // Flags are data with a latency: delay the level-1 OR by u_add_r's
  // 2-cycle latency so it retires with its own transaction.
  wire [2:0] f1 = {inv_ab | inv_cd, ovf_ab | ovf_cd, inx_ab | inx_cd};
  reg  [2:0] f1_d1, f1_d2;
  always @(posedge clk) begin
    f1_d1 <= f1;
    f1_d2 <= f1_d1;
  end
  assign invalid  = f1_d2[2] | inv_r;
  assign overflow = f1_d2[1] | ovf_r;
  assign inexact  = f1_d2[0] | inx_r;
```

Pasting chapter 10's OR in verbatim — six flops omitted, the `NOFD` mutant — is the four-input skew bug, and it is *worse* than the two-input one: **16,162 mismatches in 101,019 streamed quads (16.0 %), with every single data word correct** — 15,662 flag-only plus 500 X-class, result-changed = 0. First kill at cycle 7: the L1-overflow quad retired `7fc00000/100` where `/111` was due, its own overflow and inexact donated two cycles early to a neighbor. It corrupts nothing that result-bit checking looks at, fires only under streaming, and only when neighbors differ in flags — and it passes the held-quad foil `tb_single4` in full. Chapter 12 must treat **flags as data with a latency**, never as status lines.

**The composition matrix closes the blocking-assignment story, with a surprise.** The output-bank blocking mutant `blk2`, which passed 101,111 standalone checks, was composed into the tree — where the level-2 stage *does* consume the level-1 output banks on the same clock edge. Prediction: detonation. Measured: **PASS, all 101,019 quads** — the prediction was wrong, and the reason is the comb-cloud lag of the stage-bank section: every consumer of the blocking-written registers reads them through at least one continuous assignment (instance ports, the `f1` assign), which in Icarus does not re-propagate before same-edge readers sample. The controlled contrast that completes the matrix: compute the *same* flag OR directly inside the delay-line always block — one wire indirection removed, identical logical function — and the blocking-banked tree fails **7,973 times (7,693 flag-only, 280 X-class)** from cycle 4 on; the same direct-read variant over correct non-blocking adders passes everything, so the direct read is innocent and only a blocking-written source makes it a race. The measured rule for Icarus 13.0: **a blocking-written stage register corrupts exactly those readers that name it directly inside a same-edge always block; readers behind a continuous-assign hop never saw a corruption in 202,130 streamed transactions here.** Per the LRM both orderings are legal, so the passing configurations are legal races falling the lucky way in this simulator — which is the strongest form of chapter 3's thesis this guide can state: *simulation, at any stimulus volume, cannot certify blocking-assignment discipline; only the `<=` rule can.*

Finally, the shape comparison chapter 10 priced ("Depth, Registers, and the Third Option") gets its pipeline column, by arithmetic on the built banks rather than measurement: the pipelined tree costs 3×110 + 6 = **336 state bits at latency 4**; pipelining the sequential chain with the same 2-stage units would cost latency 6 and delay-matching shift registers for `c` and `d` of 2 and 4 cycles × 32 bits = 192 flops of pure delay, plus 18 of flag alignment — **540 state bits at latency 6**, same one-per-cycle throughput. The tree's pipeline advantage is latency *and* registers, not accuracy (chapter 10 killed that folklore), and the delay-matching bill is why accumulator-shaped designs get rebuilt as trees when they meet a clock. No physical area or frequency figure is claimed for either — the flop counts are counts of registers the RTL declares.

## Timing, Fmax, and the Epistemic Wall

**The wall, stated before any concept: no synthesis or static timing tool exists in this environment.** Chapter 9 built its version of this fence in "What Cannot Be Known Without a Synthesizer"; this section inherits it at the moment it matters most, because pipelining's entire *purpose* is a quantity — clock frequency — that only synthesis and static timing analysis can report. Everything in this section is documentation from standard texts plus reasoning from the built RTL's structure. Nothing in it is a measurement. Icarus is a simulator: a zero-output `-Wall` compile says nothing about meeting any clock, and the 101,111-pair streaming proof would pass unchanged if every stage's logic were a thousand times deeper, because delta cycles are not nanoseconds.

With that flag planted, the concepts the reader is owed, in this design's terms:

- **Critical path and Fmax.** In synchronous logic the clock period must cover the longest register-to-register combinational path plus the flop overheads: `T ≥ t_clkq + t_comb(max) + t_setup`, and Fmax = 1/T_min. The combinational `fp32_add2` chains compare + 26-position right shift + 27-bit subtract + 26-input LZC + 26-position left shift + 25-bit increment + pack; registering at the seams replaces "the sum of all of it" with "the worst single stage."
- **Which stage is plausibly the longest — reasoning, NOT measurement.** In `fp32_add2_p4`, stage 2 (align+addsub: an 8-bit exponent subtract, a barrel shifter, the 27-bit carry chain) and stage 3 (normalize: the 26-input priority LZC, another barrel shifter, a 9-bit subtract) are the plausible contenders — consistent with the FP-adder literature putting the alignment shifter, significand adder, and LZC/normalize on the critical path (Muller et al.; Ercegovac & Lang). Stage 1 (field decode, a compare, muxes) and stage 4 (a 25-bit increment and pack) are plausibly shorter. For the 2-stage version the front half chains compare+shift+subtract and is plausibly the longer — one reason the single interior cut went at the addsub seam. A real tool could prove or embarrass every sentence in this bullet; without one, they are structural arguments, and this guide says so rather than dressing them as facts.
- **Balance is the whole game.** Post-pipeline Fmax is set by the WORST stage, so an unbalanced cut buys latency without buying clock. The measurable half of balance is register cost (110 vs 284 bits); the unmeasurable half is per-stage delay. And speedup < depth, always: k stages cut the combinational path to roughly 1/k but multiply the per-stage register overhead (t_clkq + t_setup) by k, and imbalance eats the remainder.
- **Setup and hold.** Setup: data must settle before the capturing edge — violated by too-long stages, fixed by slowing the clock or cutting deeper. Hold: data must stay stable *after* the edge — a same-edge race in silicon, independent of clock speed, and NOT fixable by slowing down. The simulation analogue of a hold discussion is exactly the same-edge-driving measurements above: the testbench's negedge rule buys half a period of margin on both sides of the sampling edge, which is why this guide has enforced it since chapter 4.
- **Retiming.** Moving registers across combinational logic without changing function (Leiserson & Saxe) — a synthesis pass in real flows. This chapter already did one retiming by hand: the 4-stage design deliberately does not register the align seam, fusing align+addsub as a depth-versus-balance call. The coverage-bin finding is retiming's verification shadow: move a register, and every stage-qualified observation must move with it.
- **What a synthesis tool would report that cannot be known here:** achieved Fmax and per-stage slack; whether `lzc26` infers a priority chain or a parallel-prefix structure; carry-chain mapping of the 27-bit adder; LUT/FF/cell area, including the real cost of the 34-bit screen side channel riding every bank; whether the valid-only reset survives optimization; hold fixing, clock skew, false paths.

One measured fact does belong here, because it is the section's frame: pipelining changed **no result bits anywhere** — two depths and a tree, bit-identical to their combinational parents over every streamed check ever run. The transform is exactly "same function, later." Simulation has proven the function; only synthesis can promise the speed; and the honest sentence for a design review is this environment's own position: *we can prove this pipeline computes the right answer at some clock; we cannot name the clock.*

## Traps Beginners Fall Into

Every entry was demonstrated this session against this chapter's own designs; nothing is folklore.

1. **Testing a pipeline with held inputs.** The held-transaction schedule passed the skew mutant 20,092/20,092 and the flag-misalignment mutant 5,016/5,016 — both broken designs, both green. Holding operands across the latency window makes this transaction's wires and the last transaction's registers indistinguishable, so the entire wrong-pairing bug class is unexpressible. Stream a new vector every cycle; the same corner library that proves nothing when held kills the skew mutant at cycle 13 when streamed.

2. **Believing "it simulated fine" certifies your assignment discipline.** A blocking output bank passed 101,111 streamed checks standalone and 101,019 more composed into the tree — then failed 7,973 times when one wire indirection between writer and reader was removed, with the logic function unchanged. Both orderings of a same-edge race are LRM-legal; every green run on a blocking bank is a lucky draw, not a verdict. Write `<=` in every clocked block and stop reading tea leaves.

3. **"Resetting everything, to be safe."** The all-zeros state is an *illegal* datapath state, and the design decodes it faithfully: a full datapath reset manufactured a defined-looking `7F800000` with overflow and inexact raised on the first post-release cycle — twice in a row on the 4-stage version — where the un-reset design shows honest X. Reset the valid pipe; gate consumption on `out_valid`; leave the datapath alone.

4. **Debugging stages with un-labeled `$display`.** Same edge, same expression, two regions — chapter 4's finding ("Checking, and Making Failure Impossible to Miss"), re-measured mid-stream at this pipeline's own bank with counting operands:

   ```
   posedge t=55000: $display sees bank1=1  (inputs hold txn 2)
   posedge t=55000: $strobe  sees bank1=2
   ```

   `$display` shows the transaction *leaving* the bank (pre-NBA), `$strobe` the one *arriving*. A stage-by-stage debug log built on `$display` mis-attributes every value one stage downstream — a systematic off-by-one that looks like a design bug and isn't.

5. **Driving the DUT on its sampling edge.** Measured, not folklore: same-edge driving added a clean extra latency cycle in continuous streaming (1,997/1,997 cycles at latency 3, both drive styles) — which a wait-for-valid harness passes at the wrong latency — and in the one-pulse case **tore valid from data**: `out_valid=1` certifying `xxxxxxxx`, then the real result a cycle later. Control reaches its register directly while data lags through stage logic; the far-edge rule keeps them in the same slot.

6. **Reading latency with cursor subtraction.** The parsed VCD says it plainly: the marker's input-to-output distance is 15 ns and 35 ns on a 10 ns clock — LAT·T − T/2, because drive and capture edges are half a period apart. Count capturing posedges between the two changes (2 and 4, machine-counted); never subtract cursor times.

7. **Re-using combinational coverage bins in a pipelined design.** The renormalize bin read 14 without its screen qualifier, and 9 (2-stage) or 11 (4-stage) with the qualifier taken from the *input side* — all plausible nonzero numbers that pass an empty-bin gate; the true stage-qualified count is 12 at both depths. Every qualifier must travel to the stage where its bin samples, and every rare-path bin needs a pinned expected count, not just `!= 0`.

## What You Should Be Able to Do Now

- Turn a verified combinational datapath into a 2-stage or 4-stage pipeline without rewriting one line of datapath logic: pick cuts from a priced seam table, write each stage bank as a block of non-blocking assignments, run a valid bit beside the data, and count the register bill (110 vs 284 bits here, 2.6× for depth 2→4).
- Defend the two deliberate asymmetries of the shipped design — outputs registered, align seam *not* registered — one as interface protection, the other as a by-hand retiming that keeps the G/R convention inside a single stage.
- Reset only the control and say why, with the measurement in hand: a full zero reset manufactures a defined +inf/overflow from an illegal state (`e_big = 0 → e_norm = 486`), while no reset at all leaves `out_valid` at x and hands correctness to Verilog's x-is-false luck.
- Build a streaming-equivalence harness from its four disciplines — same-simulation combinational oracle, shared-index circular scoreboard with check-before-overwrite, X-data bubbles with per-cycle `===` valid checks, count guards including the throughput equality — and explain which mutation each discipline was proven against.
- State the pipeline's new bug class precisely — wrong *pairing* of data with transaction, not wrong arithmetic — list the five measured shapes it takes (data skew, flag mis-alignment, blocking banks, illegal reset state, valid/data tearing), and name the weaker harness each one passes.
- Re-time a coverage bin: qualify it by the *pipelined* copies of its gating signals at the sampling stage, pin it to an expected count, and quote 9/11/14-versus-12 as what happens otherwise.
- Quantify X behavior instead of gesturing at it: a majority of single-bit X hits on the registered sticky absorb into bit-correct outputs (61.4 % this run), so output X-guards are partial detectors and X-discipline belongs at injection points; a defined-wrong deposit, by contrast, corrupts silently.
- Measure latency three ways — spec-anchored fill checks, a one-pulse probe, a parsed VCD — and read a waveform's horizontal distance by counting capturing edges, knowing cursor subtraction under-reads by T/2.
- Pipeline a composition: chain the valid bit level to level, delay every lower-level flag OR by the downstream latency ("flags are data with a latency"), and price tree against sequential in delay-matching registers (336 vs 540 bits, latency 4 vs 6) without claiming a nanosecond.
- Recite the epistemic wall without flinching: simulation proves the pipeline computes the right function at some clock; naming the clock — Fmax, slack, per-stage delay, area — requires a synthesizer this environment does not have, and every timing statement in this chapter is flagged reasoning, not measurement.

## Bridge to Chapter 12

Chapter 12 ships the complete four-input adder, and this chapter has written most of its specification. The datapath is decided: chapter 10's tree, chapter 9's modules, pipelined as this chapter's `fp32_add4_tree_p` — three 2-stage units, latency 4, one quad per cycle, 336 state bits — unless chapter 12 documents grounds for the 4-stage units (latency 8, deeper stages split) that only a synthesis target could supply. The interface is decided: `clk`, `rst_n`, `in_valid`/`out_valid` with the valid pipe as the only reset domain, outputs registered, consumers gating on valid — and if chapter 12 ever resets data, it resets to a *legal* don't-care, because this chapter measured what the pretty illegal zero manufactures. The flag rule is decided and has grown its latency clause: the OR of the three operations, with every lower-level contribution delayed to its own transaction's retirement — flags are data with a latency, or 16.0 % of streamed quads wear a neighbor's flags.

The regression shape is decided too. Chapter 12's suite must be built around the streaming-equivalence harness — full-size runs fit the timeout with margin (10 s and 37 s measured) — with the corner library and chapter 10's directed quads streamed back-to-back, the four renormalize reachers carried explicitly and counted by a stage-qualified, count-pinned bin (`=== 12`-style, never `!= 0`), X-data bubbles, a drain under X, and the count guards that make a truncated run fail. `tb_single`'s shape is worth keeping in the drawer as the labeled foil it is here: five bug classes pass it, and showing a reviewer *why* is cheaper than arguing. If chapter 12 adds an enable or stall, the expectation queue needs a valid-qualified push — `tb_stream`'s `vq[]` already prototypes exactly that.

One debt rides through unchanged, and chapter 12 must be the end of the line for it. **chapter 10's D9 — the latent `exact_zero` gate in `fp32_addsub`** (not this chapter's README row of the same name) — survived chapter 10's composition campaigns behind a structural mask, and it survives this chapter the same way: `fp32_addsub` is instantiated verbatim inside every pipelined unit, and streaming equivalence inherits the mask because both sides compute the same masked function. Pipelining neither exposed nor retired it; chapter 9's unit bench remains its only witness. Chapter 12 must either resolve it or formally accept it in writing, with the structural argument attached — and either way, keep the unit suites running next to the integration sweep, because this guide has now measured masking in both directions twice.

The four-input adder you will ship in the next chapter is, in a precise sense, already sitting in `src/ch11/` — streamed, mutation-hardened, bit-identical to its combinational self. What chapter 12 adds is everything that makes a design a *deliverable*: the full written specification with port order and flag semantics pinned, the corner-case suite as a permanent regression, the coverage ledger, and the sign-off argument that says — with this guide's characteristic split — what is proven, what is accepted, and what only a synthesizer could add.
